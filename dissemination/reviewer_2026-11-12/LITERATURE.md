# Literature review: transient evidence, accumulation, and decision variability

Date: November 12, 2026. This is a targeted, critical narrative review, not a systematic review or a claim of exhaustive coverage. The question was what the present mouse motion-pulse data can add, given existing work on evidence accumulation, perturbations, alternative strategies, and behavioral state.

I used the repository's existing bibliography and notes as leads, then searched public scholarly sources for the primary papers, related counterexamples, and recent work through the review date. Full-text access and the scope of inspection are distinguished below. No unpublished data or figures were sent to external research services. `public_fulltext_access.json` records the Europe PMC retrieval outcomes. Five core full-text XMLs were successfully retrieved and their abstracts and relevant body paragraphs inspected; three other retrieval attempts timed out. Full text of the two recent eLife reviewed preprints was also inspected through the browser. Abstract/index-based entries should not be represented as full methodological replications.

## What the literature does to the proposed story

**Integration versus nonintegration is an identifiability problem with an extensive literature.** Stine et al. show that common psychometric and timing signatures can be mimicked by nonintegrating strategies. Hyafil et al. provide complementary positive tests using controlled discrete evidence: how early and late evidence combine, and whether extreme samples dominate. These papers support stronger behavioral tests, not a presumption that an integration model winning a chosen comparison establishes a unique strategy. The present dataset's temporally correlated hidden coherence complicates a direct application of independent-sample tests. [Stine 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55365), [Hyafil 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.84045).

**Pulse effects are an established perturbative tool.** Huk and Shadlen combined behavioral perturbations with parietal activity; Wong et al. showed that recurrent circuit dynamics can reproduce pulse effects and nonlinear temporal interactions. Kiani et al. demonstrated why externally available evidence can cease influencing behavior after a bound has been reached. A long-lived behavioral effect therefore has to be interpreted with decision commitment, stimulus representation, and response generation in view. Neither a declining pulse effect nor its persistence uniquely determines leak or urgency. [Huk and Shadlen 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16280581/), [Wong 2007](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.10.006.2007), [Kiani 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18354005/).

**There is substantial mouse precedent.** Visual-pulse accumulation in freely moving mice and in virtual navigation predates this project. RDK discrimination and motion adaptation, mouse visual reaction-time dynamics, and diffusion modeling also have precedents. More recently, cross-species work combines free-response visual evidence with modeling and strategy-state analyses. Continuous noisy motion with this particular signed-pulse schedule is a narrower preparation-specific contribution. I did not establish priority for that exact combination, and failure to locate an exact match is not evidence of first use. [Odoemene 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30322902/), [Pinto 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29559900/), [Samonds 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30627645/), [Chakravarty 2025 reviewed preprint](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/105116v1).

**Structured variability is a serious competing explanation.** Ashwood et al. show that temporally persistent strategies can account for patterns traditionally summarized as lapses. This supplies a concrete alternative to interpreting every failure as independent trialwise sensory or drift noise. It does not establish that the same latent states explain the present pulse data. The relevant test is whether a state/history account predicts multiple held-out signatures, especially ordinary RT serial structure and perturbation responses. [Ashwood 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-01007-z).

**The strongest recent work separates stages or tests models against additional observations.** Stine et al.'s Neuron study distinguishes accumulation and termination using population recordings and intervention. Steinemann et al. constrain single-decision dynamics rather than relying only on averaged ramps. Their shared/related macaque experiments should not be counted as wholly independent behavioral replications. These studies are useful standards for the specificity of explanation, not a requirement to collect analogous neural data. [Stine 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.05.028), [Steinemann 2024](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.90859).

**Recent counterexamples make overstatement especially risky.** A 2026 reviewed preprint finds a particular extrema model competitive with integration even after adding average EEG constraints, in a noiseless, deferred-response contrast task. Another finds different pulse-related dynamics in a transient CPP stage and a sustained motor-related stage. Neither is a mouse RDK study or a direct account of these data. Together they motivate testing where evidence is transformed, maintained, and read out rather than assigning every behavioral time course to one integrator. [Hajimohammadi 2026, v2](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/111167v2), [Parés-Pujolràs 2026, v3](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/107980v3).

## Where the present data might add something

The most interesting opening is not a claim that pulses or accumulation are new. It is a **within-dataset conflict between two constraints on the source of decision variability**: ordinary RT distributions favor a particular independent-drift-variability formulation, while perturbation choices constrain its consequences. Existing counterfactuals make that opening concrete, but do not yet distinguish a wrong variability assumption from a sensory-input or response-process error.

A convincing computational advance would show all of the following: the conflict survives assignment-based and preprocessing sensitivities; a specific compact alternative predicts several additional observations; competing mechanisms are recoverable at these sample sizes; and the prediction is robust to fitting and Monte Carlo uncertainty. A public-data test would extend the finding beyond this particular preparation without requiring new experiments. All are proposed analyses, not literature-established results for this dataset.

The most informative next empirical signatures are RT serial dependence, conditional accuracy, history-dependent bias, response occurrence, and pulse effects on cumulative response probabilities. They test an account's ordinary-trial implications in addition to the pulse residual that motivated it. A latent-state model winning choices alone would not resolve the current RT contradiction.

## Search and access limitations

Search families included: integration versus extrema/snapshot discrimination; motion pulses and perturbation persistence; leak, urgency and recurrent dynamics; mouse visual accumulation and RDK; mouse RT/diffusion models; persistent strategy states; and recent 2025–2026 integration/nonintegration papers. Public PubMed, PMC, publisher, DOI and eLife records were checked. Searches were seeded by the repository bibliography, so this is not an independent exhaustive database screening. No screening flow count or systematic-review completeness is claimed.

Publisher/PMC browser access sometimes returned bot challenges. Europe PMC provided an alternate route for the five core articles marked F. Neuron's official aims page returned an access error, so this review does not quote a purported formal Neuron selection criterion. The editorial judgment in `REVIEW.md` is based on the specificity, novelty, and inferential strength of the study relative to primary research, including the cited Neuron article.

The matrix deliberately includes both pro-integration evidence and evidence for mimicry, state dependence, and multi-stage processing. It supports neither a general rejection of accumulation nor a presumption that one of those alternatives must explain these mice.
