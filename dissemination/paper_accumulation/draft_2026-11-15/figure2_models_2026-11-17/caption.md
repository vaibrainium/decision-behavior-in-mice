# Figure 2. Candidate decision models

**(A)** Schematics of three decision rules, introducing all five tested implementations: C and DDMx (accumulation), E (rapid forgetting), and ES and ESx (extrema detection). The same constructed sequence of noisy momentary evidence increments appears above each model. C sums successive increments until a bound is crossed. E applies the same type of state update with rapid leakage toward its starting state, retaining recent evidence more strongly than older evidence. ES tests each increment against positive and negative thresholds without summing increments across time. Orange dots indicate the first positive threshold crossing; traces terminate at that crossing. The construction includes an interval with zero mean increments and a deliberately large positive increment to illustrate retention, decay and threshold detection. These are pedagogical examples, not recorded stimulus streams or fitted behavioral predictions. Decision signals are divided by model-specific illustrative thresholds; plotted time ticks are omitted because the examples do not predict biological response times. The 20-ms illustrative E decay constant lies within the fitted 5–50-ms range; schematic thresholds are arbitrary and do not report fitted bounds or threshold ranges. Shared sensory and response components are omitted for clarity; the fitted implementations are detailed in Methods. This original drawing follows the strategy-introduction approach of Stine et al. (2020), Figure 1 (https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55365). Their snapshot model is not among the matched candidates shown here. C, E and ES use reconstructed motion-energy inputs and include across-trial drift variability and fast guesses. DDMx and ESx use nominal-coherence inputs and omit those two components. The reference integrator DDMx starts midway between its bounds. One illustrative trace per decision rule is shown; grouping implementations within a family does not imply identical fitted inputs, parameters or predictions.

Source coordinates and exact construction settings: schematic_coordinates.csv and schematic_manifest.json. Control-model results are now a separate provisional Figure 3. Earlier artwork and manuscript packets remain preserved.

**(B)** Implementation comparison. C, E and ES use motion-energy inputs and include across-trial drift variability and fast guesses. DDMx and ESx use nominal coherence and omit those components. “Yes” indicates inclusion as a model component, not a claim that its fitted value is nonzero or statistically required. All candidates include within-trial noise; the variability column refers specifically to across-trial drift variability. Other parameter differences and response rules remain in Methods. Source: model_comparison.csv, Methods and implementation source hashes in schematic_manifest.json.
