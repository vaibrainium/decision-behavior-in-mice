# Proposed Figure 2A caption

**(A)** Schematics of the three matched decision rules tested on control trials. The same constructed sequence of noisy momentary evidence increments appears above each model. C sums successive increments until a bound is crossed. E applies the same type of state update with rapid leakage toward its starting state, retaining recent evidence more strongly than older evidence. ES tests each increment against positive and negative thresholds without summing increments across time. Orange dots indicate the first positive threshold crossing; traces terminate at that crossing. The construction includes an interval with zero mean increments and a deliberately large positive increment to illustrate retention, decay and threshold detection. These are pedagogical examples, not recorded stimulus streams or fitted behavioral predictions. Decision signals are divided by model-specific illustrative thresholds; plotted time ticks are omitted because the examples do not predict biological response times. The 20-ms illustrative E decay constant lies within the fitted 5–50-ms range; schematic thresholds are arbitrary and do not report fitted bounds or threshold ranges. Shared sensory and response components are omitted for clarity; the fitted implementations are detailed in Methods. This original drawing follows the strategy-introduction approach of Stine et al. (2020), Figure 1 (https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55365). Their snapshot model is not among the matched candidates shown here. Reference implementations DDMx and ESx belong to the accumulation/extrema families and are reserved for recovery/method detail.
