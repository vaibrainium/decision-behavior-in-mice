# Paired pulse psychometric panel — exploratory

Definitions: α=mean (horizontal midpoint of the logistic dynamic range), β=var (slope coefficient). No alternative answer received to the optional label clarification; this mapping was explicitly stated and matches existing pulse code’s β convention. α is not necessarily the50%-right point with asymmetric asymptotes. β is displayed on a log axis, but tested on its original scale.

The primary comparison retains the author’s four-parameter equation and weighting for individual fits. Each of10 mice contributes both directions; no mice excluded. Individual curves use6–8 coherence levels, with different observed end ranges for the two pulse directions. In several mice, missing asymptotes/flat response functions leave parameters weakly constrained: DBM12 left β reaches its lower bound, while other individual β estimates are extreme. Exported covariance conditions/bound flags document this; numerical fit completion alone does not establish parameter identifiability.

Holm-adjusted exact paired sign-flip p values are0.0703 for α and0.7031 for β. Neither is below0.05. Nine mice have the expected α direction, but there is no claim of absence/equivalence from the β result. Intervals in the source statistics are unadjusted t-based intervals conditional on the fitted coefficients, not uncertainty propagated through individual curve estimation. Sign-flip inference assumes independent mice and exchangeable/symmetric paired differences under the null; support asymmetry and weak fits limit the interpretation.

An existing project approach fixes pulse asymptotes from each mouse’s control curve. It was checked as a sensitivity analysis before inference, motivated by the visibly unstable unconstrained fits. It also yields a boundary-limited midpoint in DBM12 and changes the estimated mean shift. No condition-specific fits were silently replaced and no animals were dropped. The sensitivity is exported separately, without a second set of significance tests.

This panel is a visualization/check of fitted coefficient changes. The original assignment-based directional contrast and response-conditioned directional regression remain separate estimates of pulse use. A change in midpoint can reflect pulse use without a slope change. Do not present these α/β fits as proof that mice use/do not use pulses. Future refinement could propagate individual-fit uncertainty or use a joint/hierarchical psychometric model with justified asymptote sharing, but no such model was run here.
