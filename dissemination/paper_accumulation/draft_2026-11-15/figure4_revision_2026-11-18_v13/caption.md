## Figure 4. Brief motion pulses exert a sustained directional influence on choices

**(A)** Psychometric functions for left-pulse (blue downward triangles) and right-pulse (orange upward triangles) trials. Control trials are omitted from this panel. Points show unweighted means of per-mouse right-choice proportions; error bars show SEM across available mice. Solid curves are separate descriptive four-parameter logistic fits using the author’s logit_4 equation and default bounds, fitted without trial-count weighting across coherence levels to these equal-mouse means. Both fitted curves are displayed from −100% to +100% coherence; portions beyond the observed range of a pulse direction are descriptive extrapolations, with no additional data points. The plotted pulse conditions contain 8,529 eligible responses from 10 mice under the existing symmetric-schedule, early/middle-onset selection, with responses after the pulse ended and pulse-to-response interval at least 0.1 s. These curves are descriptive psychometric fits, not new decision-model fits or a test of their separation.

**(B)** Paired psychometric parameters for physical left- and right-pulse trials; each line connects one mouse (10 mice; all retained). Individual four-parameter curves use the same author logit_4 equation, trial-count-weighted least-squares objective and default parameter bounds. To avoid local solutions from the helper’s single start, 19 initializations were evaluated per curve and the lowest weighted residual sum of squares was selected before parameter comparisons. α is the logistic horizontal midpoint (`mean`), not necessarily the coherence giving P(right)=0.5 with asymmetric asymptotes. β is the slope coefficient (`var`), displayed logarithmically but tested on its original scale. Two-sided exact sign-flip tests enumerate all1,024 mouse-level signs of right-minus-left coefficient differences; Holm correction covers the two coefficient contrasts. Mean α difference was−22.28 coherence-percentage units (median−16.38; signed dz−1.08; unadjusted t-based95% CI−37.06 to−7.50); Holm-adjusted exact p=0.0117. Mean β difference was−1.0109 per coherence-percentage unit (median−0.0210; signed dz−0.48; unadjusted t-based95% CI−2.5139 to0.4921); Holm-adjusted exact p=0.1367. Parameter-estimation uncertainty was not propagated; several steep/sparse curves remain weakly identified. The exploratory paired result supports a directional horizontal shift but does not identify an accumulation or attention mechanism. Individual fit audits and shared-asymptote sensitivity estimates are exported.

**(C)** Directional pulse influence across early, middle and late pulse stages, coded 1, 2 and 3. A joint nonlinear psychometric model was fitted to 21,725 restricted responses, with categorical baseline offsets for 10 mice and shared pulse, coherence-sensitivity and asymptote parameters. Influence w is half the right-minus-left additive coherence-offset separation; points and line show fitted estimates, not raw observations. Shading shows pointwise 95% mouse-cluster CR1/t9 intervals using the full observed Hessian. The fitted linear slope was −5.40 coherence-percentage units per stage (95% CI −9.85 to −0.95; two-sided cluster p=0.0226; cluster-jackknife sensitivity p=0.0440). Only six mice contributed late pulses. The matched-six-mouse sensitivity analysis was inconclusive (slope −1.72, 95% CI −7.25 to 3.82, p=0.4615), so the all-available estimate depends on the shared-effect assumption. Ordinal stage is not elapsed physical time and does not estimate an intrinsic integration time constant.

**(D)** Coherence-equivalent pulse influence multiplied by mean response time from motion onset, γT. The coherence-equivalent influence γ is the separately estimated directional log-odds coefficient divided by the coherence slope. Points show the observed readout; vertical lines show percentile 95% intervals from 600 mouse-bootstrap samples. The dotted line marks the unweighted mean over the 0.3–1.2-s bins. This additional dilution-adjusted analysis retains the observed later decline. Its behavioral time course constrains model predictions without directly estimating an intrinsic integration time constant. Points in D are positioned at the observed mean pulse-to-response latency of each bin; the x axis is physical time.

The analysis uses 19,313 eligible early/middle responses from 10 mice, with matched virtual control onsets, responses after pulse offset and at least 0.1 s after onset. Late-onset strata are excluded here, whereas C includes late pulses from six mice.

Current figure: A–C preserved; dilution-adjusted influence retained as D. Separate directional panel removed; congruency ROC excluded from planned supplement. No refits or new tests.

The blue overlay in D is the saved descriptive exponential y=A exp[−(t−t_ref)/τ], with fixed zero asymptote, nonnegative amplitude and unweighted least squares across all six displayed bins. The negative late estimate is retained. τ=1.11 s (percentile 95% interval 0.45–3.73 s from 600 saved whole-mouse bootstrap curves; seven fits reach the slow-decay search boundary). Blue shading is the pointwise bootstrap fit band, conditional on observed mean time coordinates. This is an approximate description of the time course, not an intrinsic forgetting-time estimate. No new fit was performed for the overlay.

Observed points in D are connected as a visual guide; the black segments are not an additional fitted model. Panel width is reduced for author review.
