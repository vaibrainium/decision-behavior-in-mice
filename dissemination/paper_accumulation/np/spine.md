## Story (OCAR)
- **Opening (problem space, audience):** Evidence accumulation is the standard account of random-dot motion decisions, but psychometric and chronometric curves alone can also be produced by non-integrating strategies such as rapid forgetting or extrema detection (stine2020differentiating). Integration has been favored over extrema or snapshot models across macaques, humans and rats (hyafil2023temporal), and over burst-detection and precedence-detection regimes of a pulse-accumulator model in rats (brunton2013rats), and separating the strategies with response times is the approach stine2020differentiating proposed. The contribution here is narrow: mice, continuous random-dot motion, free response times, a joint held-out fit of choices and response times, and a simulation test of whether that comparison can recognise rapid forgetting. Audience: decision-making and mouse-vision researchers who model choices and response times.
- **Challenge (the hinge of the paper):** When mice discriminate continuous noisy motion, does joint held-out prediction of control-trial choices and response times favor a bounded accumulator over a tested rapid-forgetting model, can that comparison recognise rapid forgetting when rapid forgetting generated the data, and which accumulator components does the prediction need? Secondary, reported in Results and Discussion only: could scheduled motion pulses resolve how evidence is weighted over time (primacy, recency or flat; levi2018strategic, okazawa2018psychophysical)?
- **Action (one line per experiment):**
  1. Ten head-fixed mice on two rigs performed a reaction-time random-dot direction task with control trials and scheduled ±100% coherence pulses; every analysis uses responded trials only, response at 0.3–5 s (Fig 1).
  2. Each trial's stimulus was replayed and filtered into motion energy, which entered every model through a linear front end (Fig 2).
  3. A bounded accumulator and a rapid-forgetting model (leak 20–200/s on filtered motion energy), sharing input, bounds and fast-guess mixture, were compared by five-fold cross-validation on control trials with a likelihood conditional on a response by 5 s; a free-leak fit described the fitted leak (Fig 3).
  4. The same comparison was applied to data simulated from each fitted model, using each mouse's control-trial inputs and counts, to measure how often rapid-forgetting data were scored as favoring the accumulator (F1a; Fig 3D).
  5. Nested accumulator variants (drift variability, fast guesses, guess bias, leak, urgency) were compared on held-out control trials, with model recovery of the variants (Fig 4).
  6. The behavioral effect of scheduled pulse direction was estimated within matched cells among responded trials, with mice as the unit (Fig 5).
  7. The closed primacy procedure (predictive gates, gain revision, timing calibration) was re-run on responded trials as a sensitivity re-analysis (amendment 9) and reported beside its original results (Fig 6).
- **Resolution (answers the Challenge; values pending):** With the linear motion-energy input, held-out control prediction favored the tested accumulator over the tested rapid-forgetting model in [PENDING: responded-only refit] of ten mice, with [PENDING: responded-only refit] favoring rapid forgetting and the rest not distinguishing them (under the superseded censored fits, four of ten mice did not distinguish them). This ranks two models that both misfit parts of the data. Rapid-forgetting-generated data were scored as favoring the accumulator in [PENDING: F1a] of 20 datasets, which decides whether the ranking carries information against rapid forgetting (selection rule above). A descriptive free-leak fit placed leak at [PENDING: responded-only refit R5], with the leak-recovery limit stated. Under the parsimony rule the accumulator needed [PENDING: responded-only refit R2/R3; superseded fits: drift variability and fast guesses with a free guess bias, leak and urgency not needed]. Scheduled pulse direction shifted response direction among responded trials by about 7.5 percentage points (responders-only re-estimate, `/src/.agents/reports/2026-10-27-pulse-contrast-responders-only.md`). Whether the control-fitted models predict that shift, and whether the pulses could resolve temporal weighting, is reported as a sensitivity re-analysis [PENDING: amendment 9 re-run]; the original closed procedure found the pulse effect far above prediction, failed gates, an unrun timing test and no calibrated sensitivity to 10–40% primacy. Temporal weighting is reported as undetermined, not as flat.

## Competing hypotheses and what each predicts
| Hypothesis | Prediction | Which figure decides it |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Control-trial decisions reflect bounded accumulation with a long time constant | The accumulator gives a lower held-out control score than the rapid-forgetting model in most mice, and the comparison rarely favors the accumulator when rapid forgetting generated the data. | Figure 3A–B with Figure 3D (F1a). Under THESIS_B, Figure 3 ranks the two implementations only. |
| H2: Control-trial decisions reflect rapid forgetting (extrema-like; leak 20–200/s on filtered motion energy) | The rapid-forgetting model matches or beats the accumulator held out. | H4 (secondary): Early evidence is weighted more (primacy) than late or equal (recency/flat) | Under primacy, middle pulses shift choices less than early pulses once the model predicts pulse trials adequately. | Undecided. ## Key control (rules out the boring explanation)
The boring explanation for H1 is that the accumulator wins through extra flexibility, through structure from pulse trials, through optimizer failure in the rapid-forgetting fits, or because the comparison would favor the accumulator whatever generated the data. The comparison addresses these as follows: (i) both models share the same linear motion-energy input, bound structure, fast-guess mixture, response mapping and likelihood (conditional on a response by 5 s) and differ only in leak; (ii) selection used five-fold cross-validation on responded control trials, so pulse-trial choices and response times did not enter selection (the 48 historically mislabeled pulse trials all lack a response by 5 s and drop out under D003); (iii) a free-leak fit (0–200/s), which could reach the rapid-forgetting range, is an in-sample optimizer check, not a cross-validated test; (iv) **F1a comparison recovery**, the control for the last explanation: the same procedure applied to rapid-forgetting-generated and accumulator-generated data, with the decision rule fixed before the run (D002); (v) model recovery of the accumulator variants shows which components the procedure can identify. For the pulse effect, pulse direction was scheduled before the outcome and the contrast compares positive and negative assignments within mouse × session × signed-coherence × onset cells among responded trials. Conditioning on a response is conditioning on a post-pulse outcome; this selection caveat is stated, not removed. Assignment is described as scheduled, not randomized. None of these controls separates sensory, decision and response contributions to the pulse response.

## Scope: what this paper will NOT claim
- It will not analyse, model or interpret trials without a response by 5 s (no-RT trials and responses after 5 s). They are excluded from every analysis (D003); Methods reports only their count as an exclusion. Every model likelihood is conditional on a response by 5 s; no claim concerns response rate, censoring or late responses.
- It will not claim that mice learned the task or describe training; Figure 1 shows performance only.
- It will not claim that the data show accumulation, identify the mouse's algorithm, or rule out extrema detection or any other non-integrating strategy. The comparison ranks two tested implementations; under THESIS_A it recognised simulated rapid forgetting, which strengthens the ranking but does not exclude untested strategies.
- It will not describe the control comparison as testing how momentary evidence is used over time; control-trial motion energy varies little around nominal coherence.
- It will not call the rapid-forgetting model an exact extrema detector; it is an operational approximation with leak 20–200/s applied to temporally filtered motion energy.
- It will not claim the rankings are robust to the input front end; they use the linear front end.
- It will not claim leak or urgency are absent, or that adding them "did not improve" the score, whatever the refit gives; wording follows the refit numbers and the parsimony rule.
- It will not use the free-leak fit to exclude time constants of a few hundred milliseconds.
- It will not interpret the guess delay, per-mouse drift-variability presence, or drift variability as a specific mechanism.
- It will not claim starting-point variability is unneeded in the current model; it was not tested in this model generation.
- It will not put temporal weighting in the title or the abstract headline (D002 route c), and it will not claim primacy, recency or flat weighting.
- It will not present the point-3 re-analysis as the original prespecified test. The point-3 evidence is a responded-only sensitivity re-analysis of a procedure closed after its results were seen (amendment 9); the original results are also reported, side by side, with denominators stated.
- It will not present the pulse excess as a discovery about mouse sensory processing, describe the pulse–model mismatch as a "wrong temporal profile", or attribute it to sensory, decision or response stages.
- It will not call pulses "evidence" for the decision; they are scheduled motion pulses that also form a salient visual transient.
- It will not present the responders-only pulse contrast as free of selection: it conditions on a post-pulse outcome.
- It will not describe pulse assignment as randomized.
- It will not describe the response-aligned kernel result, if reported, as near-support for accumulation.
- It will not use the hidden-coherence analysis (N011–N013, N084–N087).
- It will not interpret differences between rigs, sites or cohorts; rig is a covariate only.
