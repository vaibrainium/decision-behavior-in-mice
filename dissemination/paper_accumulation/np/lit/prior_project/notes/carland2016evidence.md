# carland2016evidence

Carland MA, Marcos E, Thura D, Cisek P (2016). *Evidence against perfect integration of sensory information during perceptual decision making*. Journal of Neurophysiology. DOI: 10.1152/jn.00264.2015; PMID: 26609110. Access: PubMed abstract only.

- **Question / preparation:** Does brief-pulse efficacy change when humans adopt faster versus slower decision policies in motion discrimination?
- **Key findings:** Early pulses dominated in a fast blocked condition, whereas later pulses became effective and early pulses weakened when the same trials were interleaved to induce slower decisions; the authors interpreted this against perfect integration and in favour of strong leak/urgency. Locator: PubMed Abstract.
- **Relevance:** Direct conflict paper: pulse effects depend on policy and task context, so a control-to-pulse mismatch need not identify one universal decision mechanism.
- **Limitations for our use:** Human policy manipulation and abstract-only reading; its fitted mechanism is not automatically transferable to mice.
- **Paraphrase for C006:** The temporal impact of identical motion pulses can change with decision policy.
