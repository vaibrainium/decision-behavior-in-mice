## Key findings and relevance

- Participants used both pulses but underweighted the second; overall accuracy did not systematically decline with gap duration. Locator: Abstract; Results, Figure 1B.
- Motor-beta lateralisation maintained a cumulative decision representation across the gap, whereas CPP rose transiently for each pulse and returned toward baseline between pulses. Locator: Results, Figure 2.
- The simple bounded model reproduced key accuracy/order patterns and qualitative EEG patterns. Locator: Results, Figures 4–5.
- Version 3 explicitly compares reweighting alternatives: a P1-dependent unbounded reweighting model could qualitatively account for behavioural and CPP patterns, but the simple bounded model was favoured by BIC; more complex bound-plus-reweighting models were not supported after complexity penalties. Locator: Supplementary Note 1, Figures S15–S19 and Conclusion.
- **Relevance:** Counter/comparator showing that transient local pulse processing and sustained downstream representation can coexist; it narrows any attempt to locate the mouse mismatch in one stage.

## Interpretation limits

The evidence epochs are the task itself, not a strong perturbation superimposed on separately fitted continuous-motion controls. The model fits accuracy rather than the joint choice/RT/no-response distribution and uses grand-averaged conditions. The viable qualitative reweighting alternative and reviewer-noted single-trial EEG ambiguity prevent unique localization of the bound mechanism. Version 3 is reviewed but remains a preprint.

**Paraphrase for the paper:** Human EEG during separated motion glimpses supports transient pulse-local processing plus sustained motor-level evidence, while bounded and P1-dependent reweighting accounts remain partly confounded and were fit within the pulse task.
