# drugowitsch2012cost

**Citation:** Drugowitsch J, Moreno-Bote R, Churchland AK, Shadlen MN, Pouget A. *The cost of accumulating evidence in perceptual decision making.* The Journal of Neuroscience. 2012;32:3612–3628. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4010-11.2012. PMID: 22423085. Access: PubMed abstract; PMC full text available.

**Question / preparation / task / method / regions:** Two macaques and six humans performed reaction-time perceptual decisions. Diffusion models were combined with dynamic programming to infer time-dependent stopping policies; no single recorded region was required for the behavioural method.

**Key findings:** The abstract frames observation time as costly and derives decision policies that trade additional evidence against time cost.

**Relevance:** Methods precedent for urgency/collapsing-bound alternatives and for interpreting long/late response structure cautiously.

**Limitations stated/visible:** Normative time-cost modelling does not directly test brief pulse processing or the present fast-guess mixture.

**Claim-ready paraphrase:** Time-dependent stopping policies can arise from the cost of acquiring additional sensory evidence, providing an alternative to stationary-bound assumptions (abstract).
