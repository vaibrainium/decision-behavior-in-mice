# palmer2005effect

**Citation:** Palmer J, Huk AC, Shadlen MN. *The effect of stimulus strength on the speed and accuracy of a perceptual decision.* Journal of Vision. 2005;5:376–404. DOI: 10.1167/5.5.1. PMID: 16097871. Access: PubMed abstract.

**Question / preparation / task / method / regions:** Human observers performed visual direction discrimination across stimulus strengths. The paper linked psychometric and chronometric functions with a bounded evidence-accumulation model; no neural region was recorded.

**Key findings:** Stronger sensory evidence produced higher accuracy and faster responses; the abstract presents the chronometric function as the RT analogue of the psychometric function and fits both with the same bounded-accumulation framework (abstract).

**Relevance:** Methods precedent for joint speed/accuracy analysis and for treating choices and RTs as shared constraints on a model.

**Limitations stated/visible:** Human behavioural paradigm; abstract-level evidence does not address censored/no-response likelihoods.

**Claim-ready paraphrase:** Stimulus strength jointly shapes choice accuracy and RT, motivating models evaluated against both psychometric and chronometric data (abstract).
