# Statistics criteria commitment: results round 1

Sources used: `np/spine.md`, `np/config.md`, `np/briefs/results.md`, `review-rubrics.md`, and the `np-stats-reviewer` role guide (no file under `np/drafts/` seen).

| ID | Criterion | What I will look for | P0 — must fix | P1 — should fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Every statistical statement is complete and traceable | Each inferential result reports an approved number token, effect estimate, labelled uncertainty, `n` with unit, named test/statistic, exact p value where applicable and multiplicity adjustment. Descriptive quantities are labelled descriptive; dispersion and interval types are defined. | A number/result is fabricated, a central inference lacks its test or mouse-level `n`, or lower-level observations are presented as independent group replicates. | A statistic, denominator, interval level/type, tail, correction or token context is missing. |
| S2 | Mouse-level inference is not inflated by nested trials, cells or sessions | Group conclusions use ten mice as the sampling unit and show/describe per-mouse replication. Trial, scheduled-cell and session counts explain precision or support within mice but never inflate degrees of freedom; changing denominators are labelled. | Trials, cells or sessions are analyzed/reported as independent animals for a group claim. | The within-mouse estimator, equal-mouse aggregation, per-mouse distribution, or unit attached to an `n` is unclear. |
| S3 | The randomized pulse effect matches the assignment estimand | C005 is the within-supported-cell positive-versus-negative scheduled-assignment directional contrast with equal mouse weighting. Assignment precedes outcome, common support is explicit, and pulse-versus-control comparisons are separately qualified when their support sets differ. | The claim is based on delivered exposure, response-conditioned labels, unsupported pooling, or an indirect “significant here, not there” comparison. | Assignment/cell definitions, support/weights, control comparison, or the direct contrast needed for the prose is incomplete. |
| S4 | Pre-onset and no-response outcomes remain visible in effect and denominator | The primary contrast retains pre-onset responses and `no response by 5 s`, reports response components alongside direction, and distinguishes the analysis cutoff from task termination. Any exclusion or legacy-input restriction is stated beside the affected result. | Conditioning on post-onset response or complete cases changes the randomized estimand, or censoring is treated as ordinary missing data without justification. | Denominators, exclusion counts/reasons, scheduled-versus-historical labels, no-response mass, or outcome-dependent limitations are buried. |
| S5 | The behavioural effect leads with magnitude and multiplicity-controlled uncertainty | Figure 4 reports the mouse-level effect and confidence interval, then the named subject-level test and Holm-adjusted p value within the declared family. Robustness/estimator checks are described as such, not used to select the most favorable analysis. | A central p value is uncorrected, the multiplicity family is altered after inspection, or significance substitutes for the effect estimate. | The Holm family, raw-versus-adjusted p value, interval construction, mouse-level distribution/assumptions, or practical magnitude is unclear. |
| S6 | Pulse-aligned timing is response-associated, cumulative and pointwise | The 0.5–2-s pattern and shared endpoint are described as emitted-response timing. Cumulative carry-forward, retained pre-onset/no-response outcomes and pointwise—not simultaneous—intervals are stated; window/bin choices are presented as planned or exploratory. | The curve is called a temporal weighting/integration kernel, neural latency or evidence-persistence estimate, or multiple time bins yield uncorrected confirmatory claims. | Pointwise status, endpoint/window, multiplicity limits, denominator over time, or carry-forward interpretation is missing. |
| S7 | Figure 3 reports relative held-out model selection, not proof of a mechanism | N016 is identified as a control-only cross-validated score with direction and Monte Carlo working-threshold meaning. Selection is separated from in-sample adequacy; components are contextual rather than independent wins; the claim is restricted to the tested accumulator and rapid-forgetting implementations. | The threshold is called a population p value, fit and test data are conflated, components are cherry-picked as separate confirmations, or relative support becomes unique proof of accumulation. | Fold/mouse aggregation, Monte Carlo precision, held-out status, model scope, or residual fit limitations are unclear. |
| S8 | Recovery and fit limitations accompany the model claim | Selection/parameter recovery, false positives/confusions, boundary estimates, weak guess-delay recovery and important choice/RT/no-response residual misfits are reported beside C004 with the correct inferential scope. A non-significant or weakly identified parameter is not evidence of absence. | Failed recovery is concealed while the affected parameter supports mechanism, or in-sample fit quality is used as independent validation of selection. | Recovery denominators/decision rules, known failure rates, bounds, residuals, or their implications are too vague. |
| S9 | Figure 5's observed–predicted timing comparison stays descriptive | C006 compares common-row, identically weighted observed and predicted curves/window increments and is explicitly descriptive. No p value, CI-overlap logic or paired significance is invented; partial cumulative decline is not called a model reversal, and mismatch remains pipeline-level. | A descriptive overlay becomes a statistically supported group difference without an approved direct test, or the mismatch is uniquely localized to sensory, decision or response processing. | Common-row/weight matching, plotted uncertainty, model setting, legacy-input restriction, or the absence of a direct test is unclear. |
| S10 | C009 is a selected two-mouse diagnostic, not a cohort result | The choice-versus-remaining-objective gain tradeoff is reported only for PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, with per-mouse held-out changes and Monte Carlo uncertainty. Their informativeness/selection rule and weak gain identification elsewhere are explicit; they are not called representative. | The result is generalized to all mice, assigned a ten-mouse p value, or presented as independent confirmatory evidence after outcome-driven selection. | Selection rationale, held-out objective distinction, per-mouse replication, uncertainty or the limitations in the other eight mice are incomplete. |
| S11 | Failed gates, low sensitivity and null language obey the stopping record | Original and revised predictive-gate failures, inadequate RT-cell coverage, strategy recovery and primacy-calibration sensitivity appear in named S6 Results. The confirmatory timing test is explicitly not run. Failure to discriminate is not equivalence, evidence of no primacy, or support for either strategy. | A failed/inconclusive outcome is omitted, the withheld test is reported as a null result, or low sensitivity is converted into evidence of absence/equivalence. | Gate/sensitivity denominators, amendment order, stopping rule, power/calibration target, or confirmatory-versus-exploratory status is unclear. |
| S12 | Selection, multiplicity and reproducibility limits are adjacent to claims | Results identify which models, gains, windows and diagnostics were prespecified, cross-validated, amended or exploratory; all multiplicity families and pointwise displays are labelled. Each figure/result has a traceable method/output provenance, while unavailable data/code details remain explicit rather than promised. | Circular selection/testing, undisclosed analytic flexibility, or an invented availability/reproducibility claim supports a central conclusion. | Provenance, seeds/repetitions, software/output reference, exploratory labels, or limitations created by legacy rows and Monte Carlo error are deferred too far from the affected result. |
