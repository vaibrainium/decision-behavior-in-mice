# Criteria commitment: introduction round 1

Sources used: `np/briefs/introduction.md`, `np/spine.md`, `np/config.md`, `review-rubrics.md`, `section-specs.md`, and `formats.md` (no draft or checkpoint seen)

| ID | Criterion | What I will look for | BLOCK if | REVISE if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Locked thesis and scope fidelity | The Introduction motivates the exact control-to-pulse generalization question and keeps the tested object as the model-and-input pipeline; its answer matches the locked thesis without elevating primacy or a uniquely sensory, decisional, or response-level account. | The thesis is contradicted, materially replaced, or extended to an unsupported mechanism or universal strategy claim. | Emphasis drifts from relative control-model support plus failed pulse transfer, or an important scope boundary is blurred. |
| K2 | Natural four-paragraph neuroscience arc | Four paragraphs progress as framework/conflict → pulse assumption → preparation-specific gap → question, approach, and answer, with informative topic sentences and transitions rather than a sequence of ledger-like assertions. | A required move is absent or the argument cannot be followed without reconstructing it from later sections. | Paragraphs contain multiple competing points, transitions are abrupt, or the prose reads as a citation list, checklist, or compressed abstract rather than a cohesive Introduction. |
| K3 | Genuine model-identification conflict | The opening explains why familiar choice accuracy or temporal-weighting signatures do not uniquely identify accumulation, why joint choice and response-time distributions add constraints, and why any preference is only among the tested implementations. | Accumulation is treated as proved by behaviour, or alternatives beyond the tested rapid-forgetting approximation are claimed to be ruled out. | The conflict is a stock “remains unclear” gap, joint prediction is not motivated, or “among the tested implementations” (or an exact equivalent) is missing. |
| K4 | Pulses framed as a transfer test | Prior pulse effects on choice and response time are used to expose the untested assumption that a control-fitted pipeline generalizes to brief perturbations; timing, commitment, and policy are recognized as factors affecting pulse efficacy. Behavioural perturbation is explicitly separated from mechanism identification. | Pulses are said to independently prove accumulation, primacy, or a neural mechanism, or macaque neural findings are asserted to transfer directly to mice. | Pulse timing is presented as a simple mechanism fingerprint, the prospective transfer logic is weak, or “strong” implies physiological naturalism. |
| K5 | Bounded and field-aware mouse gap | The text positively acknowledges established mouse continuous-RDK work, discrete visual accumulation, and visual choice/response-time diffusion modelling, then states only that the exact conjunction was not found “in a bounded search.” | It makes a first-in-mice, “nobody,” or universal-absence claim, or erases established mouse precedents. | The conjunction is so vague that novelty sounds manufactured, the bounded-search qualifier is missing, or the paragraph becomes an undigested literature list. |
| K6 | Distinct H1–H3 predictions | A non-specialist can distinguish: H1, relative support for retention by the tested accumulator on joint held-out controls; H2, equal or better control prediction by the rapid-forgetting approximation; and H3, control support coupled to failure of both pipelines on transient strong evidence. | A hypothesis has no distinct prediction, a hypothesis is not resolved later, or the insensitive pulse-strategy comparison is presented as deciding H1 versus H2. | H1–H3 or the control-to-pulse question require Methods knowledge to understand, or relative model support and transfer adequacy are conflated. |
| K7 | Key control and its limit | The Introduction states that pulse direction was assigned before behavioural outcome and conveys how this guards against outcome-conditioned pulse labelling; it does not imply that randomization alone identifies the omitted mechanism. | The control is missing, described in a way that permits post-outcome assignment, or used to claim sensory/decision/response identification. | The control is too procedural or imprecise to explain the boring explanation it excludes, or its inferential limit is unclear. |
| K8 | eLife-style final-paragraph challenge and answer | The last paragraph clearly states the question and approach, then previews in 2–4 sentences the relative control support, randomized directional behavioural effect, failure of the tested pipelines to transfer, and failed predictive gates/inadequate sensitivity that led to withholding the timing test. | The last paragraph lacks the central challenge or answer, hides the negative S6 outcome, or claims a timing/primacy result that was not tested. | The preview omits a required contrast, becomes methods-heavy, or fails to distinguish behavioural pulse action from model interpretation. |
| K9 | Approved claim and citation discipline | C010, C011, C012, C015, and C019–C021 each appear once as a primary claim, use only verified source keys from the brief, and have citations that support the specific sentence; primary studies are preferred where available. | A scientific claim is unsupported, a citation is unverified/fabricated or does not support its sentence, or an approved primary claim is absent. | A claim is duplicated as primary, hedging mismatches its evidence, or reviews substitute for available original studies without reason. |
| K10 | No data and no unsupported promises | The section contains no numerical results or statistics and creates only promises delivered by the mapped Results/Discussion sections: specified held-out comparison, behavioural pulse effect, frozen-pipeline transfer, failed gates/sensitivity limits, and bounded novelty. | It introduces data, a result or mechanism not supported later, or silently promises an analysis that was withheld. | It includes unnecessary procedural detail, hard-coded values, or a promise whose destination is ambiguous. |
| K11 | Glossary precision and verb strength | The prose consistently uses tested accumulator, rapid-forgetting approximation, control trial, scheduled pulse assignment, and model-and-input pipeline (or faithful grammatical variants), with verbs no stronger than the evidence. | Terminology converts an operational comparison into proof of a true DDM, exact extrema detector, or decision mechanism. | A banned synonym or causal/definitive verb blurs the registered concept or exaggerates relative evidence. |
| K12 | Venue, tense, readability, and economy | The Introduction is four paragraphs and no more than 700 words; established knowledge is in present tense, the study preview in past tense, and sentences have clear agents, restrained jargon, natural cadence, and conclusions in stress position. | Length or structure prevents the required argument from being delivered, or prose is materially unintelligible to a broad eLife neuroscience reader. | There is throat-clearing, nominalization, repetitive hedging, buried emphasis, unexplained jargon, citation dumping, or otherwise robotic prose even when the factual content is correct. |
