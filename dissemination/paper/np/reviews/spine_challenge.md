# Spine challenge

## Verdict

No challenge is fatal if the paper is framed as a predictive-generalization and model-constraint study. A mechanism-level claim about evidence retention or the source of the pulse response would be fatal on the current evidence. The present scope block prevents that overreach, but several sentences and figure claims still exceed it.

## Strongest boring account

The control-selected accumulator captures ordinary trials better than one deliberately extreme rapid-forgetting implementation, but the pulse manipulation engages an unmodelled sensory-to-response pathway, or is represented inaccurately by the replay, so the pulse mismatch says little about evidence retention. The current evidence cannot eliminate this account. It can turn that limitation into the paper's contribution by claiming that good control prediction does not validate transient-evidence processing and by naming the tested pipeline rather than a unique mechanism.

## What a hostile eLife reviewer writes first

“The randomized behavioral effect is credible, but the manuscript presents failure of a model-and-replay pipeline as a conclusion about accumulation, while the diagnostic mechanism is unidentified and the clearest choice–RT conflict is confined to two mice from the rig with unresolved pulse timing.”

## Required revision before G1

1. Recast the thesis as a single generalization claim and replace model-only blame with model-and-input language.
2. Qualify the control result as joint held-out prediction among tested implementations.
3. Split Figure 5's group temporal mismatch from its two-mouse gain tradeoff.
4. Decide whether the unresolved hidden-coherence panel remains in the main figure.
5. Narrow response-time wording to emitted responses after logged onset and narrow replay controls to tested software artefacts.
