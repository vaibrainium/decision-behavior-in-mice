# Criteria commitment: title_abstract round 1

Sources used: `np/spine.md`, `np/config.md`, `np/briefs/title_abstract.md`, `review-rubrics.md`, `section-specs.md`, and `formats.md` (no draft or checkpoint seen)

| ID | Criterion | What I will look for | BLOCK if | REVISE if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Specific, evidence-matched title | The title is at most 120 characters, names mice, brief motion pulses and a limitation of the tested evidence-accumulation model/pipeline, and uses indicative language whose verb strength is supported by the randomized perturbation without implying a uniquely located mechanism. | The title claims a causal sensory, decision, or response mechanism; says accumulation was proved or refuted generally; or asserts an untested result. | The title is vague, omits species/system, sounds universal rather than tied to the tested model, mismatches the abstract, or exceeds 120 characters. |
| K2 | Locked thesis and sole primary claim | The front-of-paper text compresses the control-selection versus pulse-transfer contrast exactly: relative control support for the tested accumulator, a directional behavioural pulse effect, and failed generalization of the tested model-and-input pipelines. C001 is the sole primary claim. | The thesis is contradicted or replaced, support is generalized beyond tested implementations, or a new primary mechanism/novelty claim is introduced. | One side of the control-versus-transfer contrast is buried, or C001 is duplicated, missing, or attached to text that does not express the thesis. |
| K3 | Claim by sentence 3 and coherent abstract moves | Sentence 1 supplies context, sentence 2 states the transfer problem, and by sentence 3 a “Here, we…” or equivalent sentence identifies the study question/approach and central claim. Findings then proceed in a readable contrast to the implication. | A reader reaching sentence 3 still cannot tell what was tested or what the paper claims. | The opening is generic, moves are out of order, the claim is delayed, or the abstract reads as a ledger/checklist rather than a coherent eLife summary. |
| K4 | Approach and scale are concrete | The abstract names mice, the reaction-time random-dot-motion task, reconstructed input, joint held-out comparison on control trials, and randomized pulse transfer test, with study scale supplied through `{{N001}}` and a clear unit. | Species/task or the core control-to-pulse design is absent, or scale is invented/hard-coded. | The approach is too vague to distinguish the study from an ordinary model fit, `{{N001}}` lacks its unit, or methods detail crowds out the findings. |
| K5 | Relative control comparison is accurately quantified | The control result uses `{{N016}}`, says joint held-out prediction, and limits the preference to the tested accumulator versus rapid-forgetting implementation “among the tested implementations” or an exact equivalent. | The score is misreported, presented as a population test it is not, or used to prove accumulation or exclude all non-integration strategies. | Joint scoring, held-out status, the comparator, or the tested-set qualifier is missing or ambiguous. |
| K6 | Randomized behavioural pulse effect is statistically complete | The pulse finding uses `{{N051}}`–`{{N054}}` to report the directional effect, interval, mouse-scale unit, multiplicity-adjusted result and named Holm correction without converting the effect into accuracy or a mechanism. | Any numeric value is hard-coded or mismatched, the unit of inference is inflated, or the randomized behavioural association is presented as causal evidence for a specific mechanism. | The effect, interval, mouse count/unit, adjusted result, or correction is unclear, or statistical detail overwhelms the abstract. |
| K7 | Temporal description is bounded | `{{N059}}` describes when the pulse-associated cumulative response contrast emerged relative to logged onset and makes clear that this is emitted-response timing, not an integration kernel, neural latency, persistence constant, or inferential test of model–data timing. | The timing token is used to claim primacy, recency, an integration time constant, neural latency, or a tested mechanism. | The temporal statement can be mistaken for evidence persistence or a physical-frame timestamp, or its response-associated/descriptive status is unclear. |
| K8 | Mismatch, negative gates, and inferential limits are visible | The abstract states the descriptive temporal mismatch, mentions the two-informative-mouse choice-versus-remaining-objective tradeoff qualitatively if included, reports failed predictive gates/inadequate sensitivity, and explicitly says the confirmatory timing test was not run. It concludes that control selection did not validate transient-evidence processing. | Failed gates or the withheld timing test are hidden; an inconclusive comparison becomes equivalence; or the mismatch is assigned uniquely to sensory, decision, or response processes. | The negative result is buried under caveats, the two-mouse diagnostic is generalized, or the implication is stronger or weaker than Discussion D1. |
| K9 | Token, marker, and citation discipline | Every quantitative statement uses one of the declared number tokens; C001 is the only claim marker; the abstract/title/impact statement contain no citations, undefined abbreviations, invented facts, or undeclared statistics. | A number is hard-coded or absent from the ledger, a claim/result lacks its required marker, or content is fabricated. | A declared required token is omitted, C001 is repeated as multiple primary claims, or an abbreviation/technical label is not intelligible to a broad reader. |
| K10 | Natural eLife readability and economy | The abstract is 175–190 words, the title+abstract+impact package stays within 230 words, findings map in order to Figures 3, 4, 5 and S6, and the prose uses clear agents, active verbs, varied cadence and strong sentence endings. | Length or organization prevents delivery of the locked result, or prose is materially unintelligible. | There is throat-clearing, excessive caveat stacking, nominalization, repetitive sentence structure, buried contrasts, jargon, or a compressed/robotic catalogue of outputs. |
| K11 | Impact statement format and scope | The impact statement is third person, 15–30 words, and explains in plain language that held-out perturbations exposed a predictive limitation hidden by good control-trial performance. | It adds a causal mechanism, broad mouse novelty, primacy/recency, or another claim outside C001. | It uses first person, falls outside 15–30 words, repeats statistics, becomes promotional, or fails to say why the finding matters. |
| K12 | Tense, terminology, and front-matter boundaries | Established context and final implication use present tense; study actions/findings use past tense. Registered concepts or faithful plain-language equivalents preserve “control trial,” “tested accumulator,” “rapid-forgetting approximation,” and “model-and-input pipeline”; missing author/institutional facts remain outside scientific prose. | Terminology converts the operational comparison into a true DDM/exact extrema claim, or missing author/funding/repository facts are invented. | Tense shifts obscure knowledge versus findings, glossary synonyms weaken precision, or absent front/end-matter facts intrude into the abstract. |
