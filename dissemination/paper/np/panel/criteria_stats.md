# Criteria commitment: simulated panel statistical review

Reviewer: `np-stats-reviewer` (mode: panel)  
Committed: 2026-10-26, before reading the manuscript, marked manuscript, spine, ledgers, audits, figures, or underlying scientific reports  
Sources used: neuropaper `SKILL.md`; S6 workflow; neuropaper review rubric and formats; repository `AGENTS.md`; `statistical-analysis` and `scientific-critical-thinking` skill instructions

## Decision and severity rule

I will simulate a journal decision from the statistical evidence, not from topic appeal. P0 means a must-fix threat to validity, integrity, or a central conclusion; P1 means a should-fix problem that materially limits interpretation or reproducibility; P2 means bounded reporting or clarity polish. A statistical review with any P0 cannot recommend acceptance. `Reject` is reserved for central claims that cannot be supported without a different design or evidentiary plan; `major revision` for remediable P0s or several consequential P1s; `minor revision` for no P0 and only bounded reporting/clarification changes; `accept` for no material issue.

## Precommitted criteria

| ID | Criterion | What I will look for | P0 if | P1 if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Estimand and claim type | For each central result: population, condition/comparator, outcome, time/window, effect measure, direction, and whether the claim is descriptive, predictive, associational, or causal | The stated conclusion answers a materially different question from the analysis, or a causal/mechanistic conclusion rests only on association/model fit | The estimand or claim type is recoverable but not explicit, leaving scope or interpretation ambiguous |
| K2 | Independent unit and dependence | Mouse/subject as the group-level unit; trial/session dependence handled by subject-level summaries, hierarchy, clustered inference, or an explicitly descriptive presentation | Trials, sessions, neurons, bins, or simulations are treated as independent biological replicates for a group claim without valid dependence handling | Subject-level support is incomplete, sparse, or obscured despite an otherwise valid analysis |
| K3 | Denominators, missingness, censoring, and exclusions | Exact mice/trials/sessions per analysis; response/no-response treatment; duration truncation/censoring; exclusions and missing-data mechanism; consistency across Methods, Results, legends, and source records | Outcome-dependent exclusion or censoring can bias a central estimate and is neither modeled nor bounded by sensitivity analysis | Denominators or exclusion consequences are incompletely disclosed, or robustness to a plausible selection mechanism is not shown |
| K4 | Direct tests and multiplicity | Direct interaction/contrast for compared effects; prespecified multiplicity families; named correction or a justified hierarchical/gatekeeping rule; exploratory families labeled | A headline difference is inferred from significance versus non-significance, or an uncorrected multiplicity family can create the central finding | Family definition/correction is unclear or a secondary claim is exposed to material multiplicity risk |
| K5 | Effect magnitude and uncertainty | Effect estimates in interpretable units with intervals or sampling distributions; subject-level variation; uncertainty type and denominator defined | A central conclusion depends only on a thresholded p value or point estimate, with no usable magnitude/uncertainty evidence | Intervals/effect sizes are omitted for secondary inferences or uncertainty conventions are insufficiently clear |
| K6 | Null, equivalence, and calibration language | Nonsignificance distinguished from equivalence; failed gates and calibration reported; error rates and Monte Carlo resolution interpreted correctly | An insensitive or failed test is used as evidence of no effect, equivalence, model confirmation, or mechanism exclusion central to the paper | Null language is mostly bounded but still implies more resolution than the calibration or sample size supports |
| K7 | Power and sensitivity | Prospective or simulation-based sensitivity tied to the actual estimand, dependence, decision threshold, and plausible effects; no observed-power reasoning | A central negative/model-discrimination claim is made despite demonstrated inability to distinguish the relevant alternatives | Sensitivity limits are known but not carried consistently into Results, abstract, figures, or Discussion |
| K8 | Model comparison target | Training versus validation/held-out data are separated; cross-validation folds and aggregation are explicit; predictive comparisons use identical observations and an appropriate scoring rule; selection and final estimation are distinguished | In-sample fit is presented as out-of-sample evidence, data leak/circular selection is present, or models are compared on non-comparable datasets for the central claim | CV/holdout terminology, fold unit, paired aggregation, or uncertainty of the comparison is unclear |
| K9 | Model adequacy, identification, and recovery | Absolute fit/posterior predictive checks; optimizer/Monte Carlo stability; parameter recovery/identifiability; known misfit and false-positive behavior; alternate accounts | A parameter or mechanistic interpretation essential to the thesis is unsupported because adequacy, recovery, or identification fails | Known misfit/recovery limitations materially narrow interpretation but are not integrated into the claims |
| K10 | Confirmatory versus adaptive work | Prespecification/amendments, stopping rules, gate order, and exploratory departures are visible; post-failure revisions are not described as independent confirmation | Outcome-informed adaptation is presented as confirmatory evidence central to the thesis, or undisclosed flexibility plausibly manufactures the result | Adaptive/exploratory status is disclosed incompletely or inconsistently across sections |
| K11 | Controls, confounds, and transport | Control conditions address task/rig/site differences and perturbation artifacts; subgroup/site variation is not silently generalized; descriptive cross-group patterns remain descriptive unless directly tested | A central causal or comparative conclusion lacks an adequate control or rests on an unresolved confound | Plausible site/rig/task confounding or limited transportability is not sufficiently bounded |
| K12 | Numerical and cross-document traceability | Every reported number maps to the number ledger and source; same estimand has consistent values, units, denominators, interval definitions, and test labels across prose and figures | A headline value is unsupported, inconsistent, or traceability reveals a different analysis than described | Reproducibility is hindered by incomplete version, seed, software, denominator, or source-detail reporting |
| K13 | Descriptive/inferential boundary | Smoothing, binned curves, across-mouse ranges, example traces, and simulation summaries are labeled descriptive unless accompanied by valid inference at the correct unit | A visual/descriptive pattern is used as decisive inferential evidence for a central conclusion | Wording or legend design invites an inferential reading beyond the analysis actually performed |
| K14 | Conclusion follows from the evidence | Abstract, Results, title, and Discussion preserve the same bounded hierarchy: supported positive evidence, failed/insensitive tests, uncertainty, and scope | The main conclusion survives only by omitting contradictory evidence, conflating relative support with truth, or overgeneralizing beyond sampled subjects/tasks | The conclusion is directionally supportable but needs narrower wording or a more prominent limitation |

## Review procedure

1. Map each central claim to its estimand, unit, denominator, analysis class, uncertainty, multiplicity family, and source record.
2. Reconcile manuscript prose against the marked manuscript, spine, claim/number/source ledgers, integrity audit and recheck, selected figure/source manifest, and only the underlying reports needed to resolve discrepancies.
3. Treat absent reporting as unknown, not as proof a procedure was omitted; distinguish verified facts from reviewer inference.
4. Report each issue once, with severity, exact manuscript location, evidence path, consequence, and the smallest defensible remedy. Do not request new experiments or analyses when a bounded wording, disclosure, or limitation remedy is sufficient.
5. Do not alter the manuscript, ledgers, state, or gate status, and do not advance beyond G5.
