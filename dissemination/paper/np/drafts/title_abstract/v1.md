# Brief motion pulses expose limits of an evidence-accumulation model in mice

## Abstract

Within-condition prediction may not extend to transient evidence. We asked whether a model-and-input pipeline selected under ordinary evidence could predict responses to randomized strong motion pulses. Here, in {{N001}} performing a reaction-time random-dot-motion task, joint held-out control prediction favoured the tested accumulator among the tested implementations, but pulse responses exposed failed out-of-condition transfer [[C001]]. We reconstructed trial-specific motion energy, fitted accumulator and rapid-forgetting models jointly to response direction, timing and occurrence, and froze their pipelines before pulse evaluation. Rapid-forgetting minus accumulator held-out negative log likelihood was {{N016}}. Positive rather than negative pulse assignment shifted response direction by {{N051}} (95% CI, {{N052}}–{{N053}}; Holm-adjusted P = {{N054}}), while the pulse-aligned contrast reached {{N059}} at the shared endpoint. The pipelines instead predicted an early effect that weakened or partly reversed, unlike the delayed, sustained response-associated contrast in mice. In two informative mice, gains improving held-out choices worsened the remaining response objective. Both predictive gates failed, and inadequate sensitivity precluded the confirmatory timing test. Thus, model selection and pulse transfer placed different constraints on the tested pipeline, without identifying a unique sensory, decision or response mechanism.

## Impact statement

This study shows that held-out motion perturbations can expose predictive limitations hidden by successful model selection on ordinary behaviour.
