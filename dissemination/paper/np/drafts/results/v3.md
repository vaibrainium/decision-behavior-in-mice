## Results

### The experiment provided choice, response-time and no-response constraints during motion discrimination with scheduled directional perturbations [[C002]]

To obtain complementary constraints on perceptual decisions, we analysed a reaction-time random-dot-motion task in which mice reported leftward or rightward motion by licking the corresponding response port (Figure 1A). The dataset comprised {{N001}} mice across {{N004}} mouse-sessions, collected with two task implementations that shared the decision structure but used different scheduled perturbation timings (Figure 1B) [[C002]]. Responses could register after a minimum viewing interval of {{N009}} s, and the analysis classified trials without a registered response by {{N010}} s as no response by 5 s [[C002]]. This cutoff was an analysis boundary rather than a shared task-termination time: the records included later responses and trials without an observed response [[C002]]. Across the descriptive dataset, {{N005}} trials contained a response within the window and {{N006}} trials did not [[C002]]. The outcome space therefore constrained response direction, response time and response probability rather than choice alone [[C002]].

Scheduled perturbations temporarily replaced baseline motion with strong motion in one of two directions [[C002]]. Assignment preceded the behavioural outcome, but a scheduled pulse could occur after a response on the latest-onset trials [[C002]]. We therefore used scheduled assignment rather than an outcome-derived pulse label and retained assigned trials regardless of whether a response preceded onset [[C002]]. Control-trial psychometric functions related rightward-response probability to signed coherence, response-time summaries separated correct and error trials, and no-response summaries retained every trial at the fixed cutoff (Figure 1C–E) [[C002]]. Individual-mouse and equal-mouse summaries showed heterogeneity, but task implementation, site and cohort were confounded, so the displays did not support biological comparisons between rigs [[C002]]. Together, these panels defined the three behavioural constraints used in the joint models [[C002]].

The outcome summaries motivated modelling choices without serving as independent model validation [[C002]]. Correct and error response-time distributions were retained separately because similar choice proportions can accompany different timing predictions [[C002]]. No response by 5 s entered the response-probability objective rather than being discarded, because its frequency also varied across conditions and mice [[C002]]. Zero-coherence responses were shown separately in correct/error summaries because neither response direction was defined as correct there [[C002]]. The subsequent comparisons therefore evaluated whether a common implementation accounted jointly for response direction, conditional response time and response probability, rather than whether it reproduced a psychometric curve alone [[C002]].

### Stimulus reconstruction defined the model input and exposed assumptions and replay uncertainty beyond nominal coherence [[C003]]

To specify the time-varying model input, we regenerated each stimulus from its stored seed and task parameters and passed the rendered frames through causal, direction-opponent motion-energy filters (Figure 2A) [[C003]]. We extended replays through the analysis window rather than stopping them at the animal's response, then calibrated motion energy separately for the two task implementations into equivalent-coherence units (Figure 2B) [[C003]]. Mean reconstructed evidence followed programmed coherence, while trials at the same programmed coherence retained seed-dependent variation, including a persistent component created by the sampled dot directions (Figure 2C) [[C003]]. The reconstruction represented trial-specific evidence beyond nominal coherence, but it remained a model input rather than a direct recording of displayed frames [[C003]]. Its causal filtering was also an assumed input transformation, not a measure of sensory or neural processing time [[C003]].

Extending the replay beyond the observed response allowed a simulated decision to occur later than the animal's recorded response without ending its evidence stream at an outcome-dependent time [[C003]]. Separate calibration expressed the two implementations on a common input scale while leaving fitted behavioural sensitivity to the decision model [[C003]]. Neither operation established that the reconstructed frames exactly matched acquisition [[C003]]. Rather, Figure 2 makes the pipeline's evidence representation explicit so that later prediction failure can be attributed to the complete model-and-input object instead of to decision dynamics alone [[C003]].

Pulse-present and pulse-omitted replays sharing an initial seed were identical before scheduled onset, and the causal filter introduced no pre-onset motion-energy difference (Figure 2D) [[C003]]. At pulse offset, the task redrew background-dot directions, so the streams diverged and acquired trial-specific noise; their sign-aligned mean did not reveal persistent pulse-direction bias apart from a small deterministic rounding shift in specified cells [[C003]]. Labelled UCLA alternatives changed pulse-energy magnitude, while screen geometry, display-loop settings and the acquisition convention remained unresolved (Figure 2E) [[C003]]. Thus, replay constrained the tested model-and-input pipeline without establishing frame-perfect fidelity, a universal pulse-energy error bound or neural latency [[C003]].

The paired replay was a software counterfactual under a specified random-number coupling [[C003]]. Once pulse-present and pulse-omitted branches consumed different draws, their later noise realizations were not held identical [[C003]]. Consequently, the audit tested pre-onset equality, causal filtering and sign-aligned artefacts in the implemented representation, but it did not validate every physical display frame or establish that all post-pulse differences were caused by the programmed pulse component [[C003]]. The labelled alternatives exposed this residual input uncertainty next to the reconstruction result rather than treating it as a later generic limitation [[C003]].

Supplementary Figure S2 reports a historical control-labelled hidden-coherence analysis in which a pooled common-ratio model estimated a hidden-to-nominal weight ratio of {{N011}} [[C003]]. This descriptive ratio was conditional on the shared model and historical control labels and did not establish a population-wide discount; no scheduled-control-label sensitivity analysis was available [[C003]]. Because persistent within-trial evidence also prevents a time-specific choice association from isolating time-specific weighting, this analysis remained supplementary [[C003]].

### Joint held-out prediction on control trials favoured the tested accumulator among the tested implementations [[C004]]

We next asked whether control behaviour distinguished evidence retention from rapid forgetting [[C004]]. Both implementations used the reconstructed input, response mapping and fast-response mixture; the tested accumulator retained evidence to a bound, whereas the rapid-forgetting approximation operationalized an extrema-like alternative (Figure 3A) [[C004]]. Selection and testing were separated by scoring held-out control trials with a joint objective for choices, response probabilities and response times [[C004]]. Summed across folds and mice, rapid-forgetting minus accumulator negative log likelihood was {{N016}} NLL units, so the sign favoured the tested accumulator (Figure 3B) [[C004]]. The working per-mouse score threshold represented approximate Monte Carlo variability rather than a population significance test, and the joint score did not authorize separate claims for its component objectives [[C004]]. The result supported the tested accumulator only relative to the specified rapid-forgetting implementation [[C004]].

The score combined the probability of responding, response direction conditional on response and conditional response-time bins [[C004]]. Cross-validation held out control trials from the same mice and sessions rather than new animals [[C004]]. A positive summed difference therefore indicated better within-cohort held-out prediction by the tested accumulator as a complete implementation [[C004]]. It did not imply a group hypothesis test, and the displayed per-mouse working threshold was an operational reference for noisy simulated scores rather than a calibrated universal cutoff [[C004]]. Keeping these distinctions explicit prevented the relative score from being misread as unique identification of an accumulation mechanism [[C004]].

Component comparisons and final fit-quality displays supplied context (Figure 3C–D) [[C004]]. Final fits reproduced broad control-trial choice and correct-response-time trends but retained errors in some error-response-time and no-response predictions [[C004]]. Because these displays evaluated final fits on contributing control trials, they described adequacy rather than independent model selection [[C004]]. The recovery exercise comprised {{N018}} fit files, with the complete generator, replicate, train/test, per-state denominator and greater-than-threshold rule registered as {{N083}} and specified in Methods, “Figure 3: likelihood, fitting, comparison and recovery” [[C004]]. Under that design, the fast-response component had hit rate {{N019}} in its present state, and drift variability had false-positive rate {{N020}} under its absent-generator state [[C004]]. The fast-response delay correlation was {{N021}} across the pooled common-model generating and fitted sets, not a within-mouse precision estimate [[C004]]. Near-bound parameters, approximate scores and these recovery limits constrained mechanism interpretation without overturning the relative held-out preference [[C004]].

Recovery addressed both model-component selection and parameter identifiability [[C004]]. Hit and false-positive rates pooled mice and simulated replicates, so their registered dataset denominators did not constitute independent biological subjects [[C004]]. Likewise, the pooled generating-to-recovered correlation mixed animals and replicates and could remain high even when an individual mouse's parameter was imprecise [[C004]]. The poorly recovered fast-response delay and false selection of drift variability therefore limited component-level interpretation, while the held-out comparison remained a prediction result between the complete candidate implementations [[C004]].

The comparison addressed a restricted model-identification question [[C004]]. Better joint prediction by one complete implementation did not imply that every mouse or score component distinguished the candidates, exclude every non-integrating policy or guarantee transfer outside the control regime [[C004]]. The scheduled pulses therefore supplied an out-of-condition prediction test rather than a second model-selection dataset [[C004]].

### Randomized signed pulse assignment shifted response direction, and the cumulative response-associated contrast emerged mainly 0.5–2 s after logged onset [[C005]]

Control-trial selection did not establish whether the fitted pipelines generalized to a transient, strong input [[C005]]. We therefore analysed the randomized scheduled pulse assignment separately (Figure 4A) [[C005]]. Positive and negative assignments were compared within mouse × session × signed-coherence × onset cells containing both signs; cells were weighted by their smaller arm count, weights were normalized within mouse and mice contributed equally [[C005]]. Cells containing only one scheduled direction were excluded [[C005]]. The directional contrast was the positive-minus-negative change in right responses minus the corresponding change in left responses, divided symmetrically, with no response by 5 s retained [[C005]]. This common-support analysis included {{N055}} trials in {{N056}} cells, including {{N057}} pre-onset responses and {{N058}} trials with no response by 5 s [[C005]]. Retaining these outcomes preserved the assignment estimand and prevented response-conditioned pulse labelling [[C005]].

Across mice, positive rather than negative assignment shifted response direction by {{N051}} percentage points, with limits {{N052}} and {{N053}} (Figure 4A–B) [[C005]]. The complete registered inference was {{N088}}, which identifies the two-sided one-sample test, statistic, degrees of freedom, confidence level, raw p value and Holm family [[C005]]. The raw p value was therefore distinguished from the Holm-adjusted {{N054}} value [[C005]]. Separate right-response, left-response and no-response components summed to zero within mouse and indicated redirection rather than a common increase in responding [[C005]]. Timing-matched controls used recorded sham onsets for UW and outcome-independent pseudo-onsets for UCLA (Figure 4C) [[C005]]. Because the two control comparisons occupied different matched cells, their magnitudes did not test pulse-sign asymmetry, and an inconclusive secondary contrast did not establish absence of an effect [[C005]].

The Holm family contained the primary positive-versus-negative contrast and the two pulse-versus-control contrasts [[C005]]. The control comparisons described where each assigned direction lay relative to aligned trials without a scheduled signed pulse, but they were not an exact decomposition of the primary contrast because their common-support populations differed [[C005]]. UCLA pseudo-onsets were generated without using behavioural outcomes; changing their random seed altered control alignment without redefining the primary randomized contrast [[C005]]. The signed assignment comparison therefore remained the primary behavioural result, while control comparisons described outcome components and alignment sensitivity [[C005]].

Assignment was verified from the available task-code snapshots and a pre-outcome balance audit, but code versions were not pinned to each collection session [[C005]]. The primary behavioural estimand retained assigned outcomes; by contrast, the legacy DDM inputs excluded {{N070}} and historically mislabelled the UCLA no-response pulse trials summarized in {{N071}} [[C005]]. Those input limitations did not alter the assignment-based behavioural contrast, but they qualified the later legacy model overlay [[C005]].

We next computed cumulative right-response, left-response and no-response probabilities after logged onset (Figure 4D) [[C005]]. Across all scheduled trials, the directional contrast reached {{N059}} percentage points at the shared endpoint [[C005]]. The registered curve grid, endpoint, mouse denominator and pointwise, not simultaneous, Student-t interval convention were {{N090}} [[C005]]. The curve remained small in the earliest window and increased mainly from 0.5 to 2 s, when many responses were emitted [[C005]]. These descriptive windows were not a multiplicity-corrected early-versus-late test [[C005]]. Because cumulative curves carry earlier differences forward, they locate response-associated mass rather than evidence entry, persistence, an integration kernel, neural latency or a processing stage [[C005]].

Right and left responses were treated as competing outcomes, and trials without either response remained in the no-response probability [[C005]]. The display did not select only eventual responders or condition on survival beyond onset [[C005]]. A late cumulative contrast could therefore reflect an earlier difference carried forward, and a small early contrast could reflect the limited number of responses emitted in that interval [[C005]]. Interpretation was consequently restricted to the timing of response-associated divergence under assignment [[C005]].

### At the group level, the tested model-and-input pipelines predicted a different temporal profile from the observed pulse-associated response contrast [[C006]]

To test predictive transfer, we compared observed and simulated pulse-aligned curves on the historical model-input rows intersected with the behavioural common-support set, using identical cells and mouse weights (Figure 5A–B) [[C006]]. The overlap excluded the disclosed outcome-dependent legacy rows and retained the historical UCLA no-response pulse labels rather than silently modifying fitted inputs [[C006]]. With control parameters fixed, stored joint pulse gains and gains chosen to improve pulse choices placed more of the contrast early and produced a later partial decline, whereas the observed group contrast emerged mainly from 0.5 to 2 s and remained positive [[C006]]. Intervals described between-mouse variation within observed or predicted quantities, not a paired inferential test of observed-minus-model differences [[C006]]. The overlay also inherited finite-simulation uncertainty [[C006]]. Thus, the tested model-and-input pipelines failed to generalize their temporal profile without localizing the error to sensory encoding, decision dynamics or response generation [[C006]].

Using the same rows and weights made the observed and predicted curves comparable at the plotted-estimand level [[C006]]. It did not repair the historical labels, integrate parameter uncertainty or turn visual profile separation into a paired significance test [[C006]]. Pulse gain rescaled the reconstructed pulse component while leaving control parameters fixed [[C006]]. The persistence of the temporal-shape mismatch across displayed gains therefore constrained amplitude-only revision within this pipeline, but it did not exclude a different input representation, model form or response mapping [[C006]].

Gain profiles clarified why amplitude rescaling did not resolve the mismatch (Figure 5C–D) [[C009]]. In PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, selecting gain to improve held-out choices reduced choice NLL by {{N063}} and {{N064}} units relative to the joint-gain solution, but increased the remaining response and response-time objective by {{N065}} and {{N066}} units [[C009]]. The gain-search and diagnostic settings were {{N093}}; each dense-mouse diagnostic therefore used only its registered main Monte Carlo seed [[C009]]. These QMLE score changes retained the original objective weights, were not calibrated significance statistics and had no direct repeated-seed precision estimate for PSUIM6 or PSUIM7 [[C009]]. Choice profiles were flatter and gain estimates weakly identified in most other mice [[C009]]. C009 therefore supplied a subordinate two-mouse diagnostic for the C006 mismatch, not a cohort-wide conflict or unique mechanism [[C009]].

The objective decomposition linked the choice and temporal-profile diagnostics [[C009]]. In the two named mice, gains that improved the final choice component also strengthened predicted response timing patterns penalized by the remaining objective [[C009]]. The limited seed basis means the numerical changes cannot supply their own Monte Carlo confidence interval, although their held-out signs support the descriptive tradeoff in these mice [[C009]]. Mice with sparse response-time contributions or flat choice profiles could not establish the same relation [[C009]].

### The original and gain-revised predictive gates failed, sensitivity was low, and the confirmatory timing test was not run [[C007]]

Supplementary Figure S6 preserves the failures that bounded interpretation. The primary baseline was the tested accumulator pipeline; the secondary `L1U1V1Z0G1` baseline added leak and urgency within the same model-and-input framework [[C007]]. In the original predictive gates, observed-minus-predicted pooled choice departure toward the pulse was {{N026}} logit for the primary baseline and {{N027}} logit for the secondary baseline, and response-time coverage fell below the registered requirement for both [[C007]]. The gate criteria were {{N095}} and constituted an adequacy screen rather than an equivalence test [[C007]]. A subsequent revision, explicitly prompted by failure, fitted one pulse gain per mouse by cross-validation while keeping control parameters fixed [[C007]]. Revised choice departures remained {{N030}} and {{N034}} logit, while fractions {{N035}} and {{N036}} of mouse-by-quantile response-time cells lay inside simulated ranges for the primary and secondary baselines, respectively [[C007]]. Choice departure measured residual calibration toward scheduled pulse direction; response-time coverage measured cells inside model simulations [[C007]]. Both gain-revised baselines therefore failed the combined gate [[C007]]. Cross-validation prevented own-trial gain fitting but could not restore independence from the observed failure that motivated revision [[C007]].

The original gates asked whether frozen control-selected predictions were sufficiently adequate on pulse-trial choices, response times and no-response probabilities to support a later timing contrast [[C007]]. Their failure stopped that confirmatory path before any early-versus-middle timing coefficient was interpreted [[C007]]. The gain revisions then asked whether one time-invariant scalar per mouse could repair pulse magnitude without changing the control models [[C007]]. Because those adapted pipelines also failed their combined adequacy screens, neither became a replacement confirmatory baseline [[C007]].

Separate simulations quantified two distinct sensitivity limits. The frozen strategy procedure produced the exact correct-verdict counts and per-generator denominators in {{N097}} under the pulse-specific rule; low sensitivity was not evidence of equivalence [[C007]]. The gain-revised timing calibration design, including fixed fitted control parameters and the per-condition denominator, was {{N096}} [[C007]]. Its exact decay-level detection mapping was {{N098}}, which also makes the coarse empirical resolution explicit [[C007]]. Strategy recovery and decay detection answered different questions and were not pooled [[C007]].

The strategy simulation conditioned on the fitted candidates and fixed frozen predictions, whereas timing calibration ran the amended gain-fitting, gate and timing procedure under imposed decay levels [[C007]]. Neither exercise integrated uncertainty in the fitted control parameters [[C007]]. Their empirical rates therefore described the tested procedures under those generators, not general power against every form of rapid forgetting or primacy [[C007]]. Reporting the registered levels and denominators made that limited resolution visible [[C007]].

Because the gates failed and calibration showed inadequate sensitivity, the confirmatory timing test was not run and the prespecified stopping rule ended further baseline revision [[C007]]. This was not a null result for primacy, recency or flat temporal weighting [[C007]]. Instead, the failed transfer and insensitive timing procedure left the source of the pulse-associated mismatch unresolved for Discussion [[C007]].
