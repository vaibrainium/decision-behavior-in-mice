## Figure legends

### Figure 1. The experiment provided choice, response-time and no-response constraints during motion discrimination with scheduled directional perturbations [[C002]]

**(A)** Task schematic. Head-fixed mice viewed random-dot motion and reported its direction at left or right response ports; responses could register after the {{N009}}-s minimum viewing interval [[C002]]. **(B)** Trial timelines for control trials and positive or negative scheduled pulses, shown separately for the two task implementations and onset schedules [[C002]]. **(C)** Rightward-response probability versus signed coherence on control trials, conditional on response by the analysis cutoff [[C002]]. **(D)** Within-mouse median response times for correct and error responses versus absolute coherence; zero-coherence responses are displayed separately because neither direction is correct [[C002]]. **(E)** Probability of no response by 5 s versus coherence [[C002]]. The {{N010}}-s cutoff is an analysis boundary, not a common task-termination time; the no-response category includes later responses and trials without an observed response [[C002]]. Thin traces or points denote individual mice and emphasized symbols denote equal-mouse means; intervals are two-sided Student-t confidence intervals across contributing mice [[C002]]. The descriptive dataset comprised {{N001}} mice, {{N004}} mouse-sessions, {{N005}} within-window responses and {{N006}} trials with no response by 5 s [[C002]]. Denominators for each plotted coherence and outcome are given in the source-data table; panels do not compare rigs inferentially [[C002]].

### Figure 2. Stimulus reconstruction defined the model input and exposed assumptions and replay uncertainty beyond nominal coherence [[C003]]

**(A)** Reconstruction pipeline from trial seed and task parameters through rendered random-dot frames, causal direction-opponent filtering and spatial pooling [[C003]]. **(B)** Time-averaged motion energy versus programmed coherence for each task implementation after separate calibration to equivalent-coherence units [[C003]]. Points summarize trial seeds, the identity line marks exact agreement with programmed coherence and dispersion shows variation across trials rather than mice [[C003]]. **(C)** Trial-specific reconstructed evidence at a common nominal coherence, illustrating the persistent seed-dependent component retained by the task's sampled dot directions [[C003]]. **(D)** Pulse-present minus physically pulse-omitted motion energy for paired same-seed replays aligned to scheduled onset and offset [[C003]]. Pulse-present and pulse-omitted streams share pre-onset frames, then diverge when the task consumes different random draws; trial bands therefore characterize the replay coupling and do not estimate group uncertainty [[C003]]. **(E)** Pulse-aligned reconstructed input under the original and labelled alternative UCLA replay conventions [[C003]]. Curves are expressed in equivalent-coherence units; bands summarize trials or seeds as identified in the panel, and the number of replays per implementation is reported in its source-data table [[C003]]. The alternatives test replay assumptions but neither identify the acquisition convention nor provide a frame-perfect record of the display [[C003]]. Temporal-filter delay belongs to the assumed input representation and is not neural latency [[C003]].

Stimulus-level panels use trials or seeds as their denominators, whereas any accompanying behavioural coefficient uses mice as its denominator [[C003]]. Error representations are labelled per panel because trial dispersion, replay uncertainty and across-mouse uncertainty are not interchangeable [[C003]].

### Figure 3. Joint held-out prediction on control trials favoured the tested accumulator among the tested implementations [[C004]]

**(A)** Schematics of the tested accumulator and rapid-forgetting approximation, both driven by reconstructed motion energy and coupled to the same fast-response mixture and response mapping [[C004]]. **(B)** Per-mouse held-out score difference on control trials, defined as rapid-forgetting minus accumulator negative log likelihood; positive values favour the tested accumulator [[C004]]. The summed difference is {{N016}} NLL units [[C004]]. The indicated working threshold describes approximate Monte Carlo score variability and is not a population significance cutoff [[C004]]. **(C)** Held-out score changes for removing or adding model components, with the displayed sign defined locally and kept consistent with panel B [[C004]]. These component scores provide model-selection context but are not independent tests of choice and response time [[C004]]. **(D)** Descriptive final-fit quality on control trials for response direction, correct and error response times and probability of no response by 5 s [[C004]]. Observed mouse summaries are overlaid with predictions from final fits; uncertainty conventions and trial counts are identified separately for each outcome in the source data [[C004]]. Panels B–C use held-out control scores, whereas panel D evaluates final fits on contributing trials and is not an independent validation [[C004]]. All panels cover {{N001}} mice; complete recovery, individual fit checks and parameter-bound flags are in Supplementary Figure S3 [[C004]].

Score bars aggregate the held-out objective over its stated folds; they are NLL differences, not effect sizes or test statistics [[C004]]. Model curves in D include the response-probability mass so that no response by 5 s is evaluated alongside choice and response time [[C004]].

### Figure 4. Randomized signed pulse assignment shifted response direction, and the cumulative response-associated contrast emerged mainly 0.5–2 s after logged onset [[C005]]

**(A)** Per-mouse directional contrast for positive versus negative scheduled pulse assignment. The contrast is the positive-minus-negative change in right responses minus the corresponding change in left responses, divided symmetrically [[C005]]. The equal-mouse mean is {{N051}} percentage points, with two-sided Student-t confidence interval {{N052}} to {{N053}} across {{N001}} mice and Holm-adjusted p value {{N054}} in the declared three-comparison family [[C005]]. **(B)** Right-response, left-response and no-response-by-5-s components of the same assignment contrast; the three outcome differences sum to zero within mouse [[C005]]. **(C)** Directional contrasts for positive pulses versus timing-matched controls and controls versus negative pulses [[C005]]. UW controls use recorded sham onsets and UCLA controls use outcome-independent pseudo-onsets; the two comparisons use different supported cells, so their magnitudes do not test pulse-sign asymmetry [[C005]]. In A–C, small points show mice and emphasized points with bars show equal-mouse means and two-sided Student-t intervals; the stated p values use Holm correction across the primary and two control contrasts [[C005]].

**(D)** Cumulative directional contrast after scheduled onset for all scheduled trials, with right and left responses treated as competing outcomes and no response carried as the remaining probability [[C005]]. The endpoint contrast is {{N059}} percentage points [[C005]]. The band is a pointwise, not simultaneous, Student-t interval across mice and carries no multiplicity-corrected early-versus-late test [[C005]]. The curve locates emitted responses associated with assignment; it is not an integration kernel or neural-latency estimate [[C005]]. Analyses used {{N055}} trials in {{N056}} supported mouse × session × signed-coherence × onset cells, including {{N057}} pre-onset responses and {{N058}} trials with no response by 5 s [[C005]]. Cells were weighted by their smaller compared-arm count, weights were normalized within mouse and mice contributed equally; cells without both pulse signs were excluded [[C005]].

### Figure 5. At the group level, the tested model-and-input pipelines predicted a different temporal profile from the observed pulse-associated response contrast [[C006]]

**(A)** Observed cumulative directional contrast and predictions from the primary model-and-input pipeline at the original, stored joint and choice-optimizing pulse gains [[C006]]. Curves use the same legacy-input trial rows, supported cells and equal-mouse weights; control parameters remain fixed [[C006]]. **(B)** Observed and predicted changes in directional contrast within successive post-onset windows [[C006]]. Points show equal-mouse means and bands or bars show two-sided Student-t intervals across mice for each quantity; intervals are descriptive and are not a paired test of observed-minus-predicted profiles [[C006]]. A negative late predicted increment denotes partial decline from an earlier cumulative contrast, not reversal of the final response direction [[C006]]. Panels A–B include {{N001}} mice and inherit both finite-simulation uncertainty and the legacy input's historical pulse-label omissions [[C006]].

**(C)** In-sample choice and joint-objective profiles over pulse gain for PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, selected because dense response-time coverage supported diagnosis, not because they represent the cohort [[C009]]. **(D)** Held-out score changes when gain was chosen on the choice objective rather than the joint objective [[C009]]. For PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, choice NLL improved by {{N063}} and {{N064}} units while the remaining response and response-time objective worsened by {{N065}} and {{N066}} units [[C009]]. Score components retain their original objective weights and are diagnostic units, not calibrated significance statistics [[C009]]. Full gain profiles and the secondary pipeline are in Supplementary Figure S5; weak identification in most other mice precludes a cohort-wide gain-tradeoff claim [[C009]].

The group panels and the selected-mouse panels have different units of inference: A–B summarize equal-weighted mice, whereas C–D diagnose objective profiles within each named mouse [[C006]]. No visual overlap or separation of their intervals is used as a significance comparison [[C006]].

### Supplementary Figure S6. The original and gain-revised predictive gates failed, sensitivity was low, and the confirmatory timing test was not run [[C007]]

**(A)** Original observed-minus-predicted pooled choice departures toward scheduled pulse direction for the primary and secondary pipelines, {{N026}} and {{N027}} logit, respectively [[C007]]. **(B)** Original response-time and no-response predictive coverage, defined as the fraction of mouse-level outcome summaries inside model simulations; the combined predictive gates failed [[C007]]. **(C)** Cross-validated pulse-gain revision with control parameters fixed. Revised choice departures are {{N030}} and {{N034}} logit, and fractions {{N035}} and {{N036}} of mouse-by-quantile response-time cells fall inside the simulated ranges for the primary and secondary pipelines [[C007]]. **(D)** Frozen strategy sensitivity: {{N024}} extrema-generated and {{N025}} accumulator-generated full-cohort datasets yielded the correct pulse-specific verdict [[C007]]. Low sensitivity does not establish strategy equivalence [[C007]]. **(E)** Gain-revised timing calibration: detections occurred in {{N039}}, {{N040}} and {{N041}} simulated datasets at the tested increasing decay levels [[C007]]. Bars count datasets within each separately labelled simulation condition; denominators and gate-pass counts are printed in the panel source data [[C007]]. **(F)** Analysis sequence and stopping decision. Both predictive gates failed, the pulse strategy comparison was not adequately sensitive, and the confirmatory timing test was not run [[C007]]. This record is not evidence of no primacy, recency or flat temporal weighting [[C007]].

Panels D and E answer different calibration questions: strategy recovery concerns discrimination between frozen candidate predictions, whereas decay detection concerns the sensitivity of the gain-revised timing procedure [[C007]]. Their denominators and verdict rules are therefore displayed separately and are not combined into one power estimate [[C007]].
