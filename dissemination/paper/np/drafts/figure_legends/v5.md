## Figure legends

### Figure 1. The experiment provided choice, response-time and no-response constraints during motion discrimination with scheduled directional perturbations [[C002]]

**(A)** Head-fixed mice viewed dynamic random-dot motion and reported leftward or rightward motion at two lick ports.
Responses could register after the {{N009}} minimum viewing interval [[C002]]. The {{N010}} cutoff is an analysis
boundary, not a shared task-termination time [[C002]]. **(B)** Scheduled replacement of the baseline stimulus by
±100% coherence. UCLA pulses occurred at early and middle onsets (medians 61 and 241 ms; median duration 51 ms); UW
pulses occurred at 50, 233 and 417 ms (duration 67 ms). Fine horizontal lines show the observed UCLA onset ranges
[[C002]]. **(C)** Rightward-response probability on scheduled-control trials, conditional on response by 5 s. Grey
traces are mice; black points and bars are the equal-mouse mean ± SEM [[C002]]. **(D)** Within-mouse median response
time for correct and error responses versus absolute coherence. At zero coherence all responses are shown separately.
A mouse entered a cell with at least 10 responses; all 10 mice met this threshold at every displayed point. Thin traces
show mice and emphasized symbols show equal-mouse mean ± SEM [[C002]]. **(E)** Probability of no response by 5 s on
all scheduled-control trials. Green circles and purple squares identify UCLA and UW mice; black points and bars are
equal-mouse mean ± SEM across all 10 mice [[C002]]. Exact panel-level trial counts and display rules are registered in
{{N105}}. The complete dataset comprised {{N001}}, {{N004}}, {{N005}} with a response in the window and {{N006}} with no response by 5 s [[C002]]. Panels C–E are descriptive and use mice, not trials or sessions, for group
uncertainty. No asterisks are used.

### Figure 2. Stimulus reconstruction defined the model input and exposed replay uncertainty beyond nominal coherence [[C003]]

**(A)** Reconstruction pipeline from logged seed and task parameters through rendered 60-Hz dot frames, causal
direction-opponent filtering, spatial averaging and rig-specific calibration to equivalent-coherence units [[C003]].
The replay is model-derived and is not claimed to be a frame-perfect display record. **(B)** Motion energy averaged
over 0.33–5 s versus programmed coherence on responded scheduled-control trials. Symbols and bars are trial mean ± SD;
the dotted line is identity [[C003]]. **(C)** Distribution of the same 0.33–5-s average on nominal 0% control trials.
Small points are trials, violins show their distributions, and open symbols and bars are trial mean ± SD [[C003]].
**(D)** Paired, same-seed replay difference between pulse-present and physically pulse-omitted stimuli, multiplied by
scheduled pulse sign and aligned to logged onset. Lines are trial means and bands are pointwise 95% Student-t intervals
[[C003]]. The streams are identical before onset and diverge after the task consumes different random draws; later
dispersion therefore includes random-stream divergence. The grey span covers the longer, four-frame pulse window.
**(E)** UCLA pulse energy over 0–333 ms under the stored replay and three labelled alternatives. Points and bars are
trial means and 95% Student-t intervals; annotations give ratios to the stored replay [[C003]]. These alternatives probe
assumptions and do not identify acquisition settings. Exact panel denominators and dispersion conventions are
registered in {{N107}}; the paired-audit design is {{N099}}. Panels B–E describe stimulus or replay variation, not
behavioral uncertainty across mice; temporal-filter delay is not neural latency. No asterisks are used.

### Figure 3. Joint held-out prediction on control trials favoured the tested accumulator among the tested implementations [[C004]]

**(A)** Schematic of the two candidate control-trial pipelines. Both receive reconstructed motion energy and share the
response bounds, fast-response mixture and mapping to choice, response time and no response; the tested distinction is
the tested accumulator versus the rapid-forgetting approximation (leak 20–200 s⁻¹) [[C004]]. **(B)** Per-mouse held-out control score,
rapid-forgetting-approximation minus tested-accumulator negative log likelihood (NLL), summed over five control-only cross-validation folds.
Positive values favour the tested accumulator. The sum was {{N016}}. Dashed lines show ±10 as a working Monte
Carlo-resolution threshold, not a confidence interval or population test [[C004]]. **(C)** Held-out changes when drift
variability or the fast-response component was removed from the strict accumulator, or leak and urgency terms were
added. Open circles are mice, diamonds are equal-mouse means and annotations are sums across mice. Positive values
favour the strict model over the named comparator [[C004]]. **(D)** Descriptive fit quality of the final all-trial
strict model evaluated on control trials. Points and error bars are equal-mouse observed means ± SEM; lines are
equal-mouse means from 20 fixed-seed simulations per contributing trial. Error-response-time cells enter only when the
corresponding observed mouse-by-coherence cell contains at least five errors. Exact observed denominators and simulation
mapping are registered in {{N106}}. Panel D is in-sample fit quality and is not part of the held-out comparison in
B–C. Simulated repetitions are not biological replicates. No asterisks are used.

### Figure 4. Randomized signed pulse assignment shifted response direction, and the cumulative response-associated contrast emerged mainly 0.5–2 s after logged onset [[C005]]

**(A)** Directional contrast for positive versus negative scheduled pulse assignment in each mouse,
`(ΔP(right) − ΔP(left))/2`. The equal-mouse mean was {{N051}} with confidence limits {{N052}}
and {{N053}}; test, statistic, degrees of freedom, raw p value and Holm-family details are {{N088}} [[C005]]. Squares
and circles identify UW and UCLA mice. **(B)** Changes in right-response, left-response and no-response-by-5-s
probabilities. Thin lines connect the three components from each mouse; coloured points and bars are equal-mouse means
and 95% Student-t intervals. The three components sum to zero within mouse [[C005]]. **(C)** Directional contrasts for
positive pulses versus timing-matched controls and controls versus negative pulses. UW controls use recorded sham times
and UCLA controls use outcome-independent pseudo-onsets; the comparisons use different supported cells and do not
estimate pulse-sign asymmetry [[C005]]. **(D)** All-scheduled cumulative directional contrast after logged pulse onset.
Thin lines are mice, the emphasized line is their equal-weight mean and the band is the pointwise, not simultaneous,
95% Student-t interval. The endpoint was {{N059}}. The analysis used {{N055}} across {{N056}} with both signs represented, retaining {{N057}} that preceded logged onset and {{N058}} with no response by 5 s
[[C005]]. Cells used smaller-arm weights normalized within mouse; mice were equal-weighted. The curve locates emitted
responses and is not a neural latency, memory kernel or confirmatory timing/primacy test. No asterisks are used.

### Figure 5. The tested model-and-input pipelines predicted a different temporal pulse profile from the observed response contrast [[C006]]

**(A)** Ten-mouse mean cumulative directional contrast on the common historical model-input rows: observed responses
and primary-pipeline predictions at the original gain of one, stored joint-objective gain and choice-optimizing gain.
Control parameters were fixed [[C006]]. **(B)** Changes within 0–0.5, 0.5–1, 1–2 and 2–4.58 s after onset. Points and
bars are equal-mouse means and 95% Student-t intervals. A negative late increment is a partial decline from an earlier
cumulative contrast, not an overall reversal of the pulse effect [[C006]]. **(C)** In-sample choice and joint-objective
profiles for PSUIM6 and PSUIM7 over gains 0–10. Each curve is shown as ΔNLL from its minimum divided by its displayed
range so both objectives remain visible; normalization does not change the optima. These mice were selected for dense
response-time coverage, not as representative examples [[C009]]. **(D)** Held-out NLL change when fold-specific gain
was chosen for choice rather than the joint objective. Negative choice-term values are improvements; positive
remaining-objective values are costs. Filled markers identify the two mice meeting the prespecified tradeoff criterion:
choice improved by {{N063}} and {{N064}} while the remaining objective worsened by {{N065}} and {{N066}}
[[C009]]. Panels A–B are descriptive group comparisons rather than paired observed-minus-predicted tests; C is
in-sample, and the QMLE changes in D do not have a calibrated inferential threshold. Historical label omissions remain
unchanged, and overlays use only the observed–predicted common intersection. No asterisks are used.

### Supplementary Figure S6. The original and gain-revised predictive gates failed, sensitivity was low, and the confirmatory timing test was not run [[C007]]

toward scheduled pulse direction, {{N026}} for the primary baseline and {{N027}} for the secondary baseline.
**(B)** Original primary and secondary response-time and no-response predictive coverage. The gate criteria and
simulated interval level are {{N095}}, and the exact coverage counts in B–C are {{N108}}. **(C)** Cross-validated pulse-gain revision with control parameters fixed: choice
departures were {{N030}} and {{N034}}; coverage was {{N035}} and {{N036}} of mouse-by-quantile response-time cells
inside simulated ranges for the primary and secondary baselines. **(D)** Frozen strategy sensitivity; exact correct
verdict counts and full-cohort denominators are {{N097}}. **(E)** Timing calibration; exact detections and per-condition
denominators are {{N098}}. **(F)** Analysis sequence. Both original and gain-revised baselines failed, sensitivity was
low and the confirmatory timing test was not run [[C007]]. Panels D and E answer distinct calibration questions and are
not pooled. These results are not evidence of no primacy, recency or flat weighting. No asterisks are used.
