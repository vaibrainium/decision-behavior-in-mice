## Themes

1. **Joint prediction is more diagnostic than familiar signatures.** Choice accuracy, apparent flat
   weighting and buildup can be reproduced by competing models; accuracy and full response-time
   distributions impose stronger, though still implementation-specific, constraints
   (`stine2020differentiating`, `ratcliff2008diffusion`, `palmer2005effect`,
   `hawkins2015discriminating`).
2. **Pulses are informative in relation to model separability and preparation.** Brief evidence has
   constrained accumulator dynamics, including through recent positive macaque neural evidence, but
   discrimination depends on timing, noise, policy, commitment and the compared predictions
   (`huk2005neural`, `stine2023termination`, `steinemann2024direct`, `zhou2009time`,
   `carland2016evidence`).
3. **Temporal weighting belongs to the full sensory-to-response pipeline.** Sensory dynamics,
   strategic reweighting, decision bounds, urgency and motor delay can all shape behavioural timing
   (`yates2017functional`, `levi2018strategic`, `okazawa2018psychophysical`, `thura2012urgency`,
   `parespujolras2026glimpses`).
4. **The literature contains a real mechanistic conflict.** Some comparisons favour bounded diffusion
   or integration, whereas others favour leak/urgency under changed evidence or policy; no one model
   wins independently of task and implementation. Recent macaque neural results provide strong positive
   accumulation evidence in a different preparation (`hyafil2023temporal`, `stine2023termination`,
   `steinemann2024direct`, `evans2017computations`, `cisek2009decisions`, `carland2016evidence`).
5. **Input and parameter validation remain part of the inference.** Motion-energy filtering supplies a
   model input but not display verification, and fitted parameters should be accompanied by recovery or
   identifiability checks before biological interpretation (`adelson1985spatiotemporal`,
   `wiecki2013hddm`, `luken2025parameter`).
6. **The mouse components are established separately.** Continuous noisy RDK discrimination, dynamic
   discrete visual evidence accumulation, and visual choice/RT diffusion modelling all precede this
   work (`douglas2006motion`, `stirman2016touchscreen`, `marques2018mousev1`,
   `pinto2018visualpulses`, `odoemene2018visual`, `koay2020amplitude`, `caballero2022rt`). The bounded
   novelty question concerns their combination with a frozen control-only strong-pulse transfer test.
