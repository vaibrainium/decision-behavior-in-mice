# hawkins2015revisiting

**Citation:** Hawkins GE, Forstmann BU, Wagenmakers EJ, Ratcliff R, Brown SD. *Revisiting the evidence for collapsing boundaries and urgency signals in perceptual decision-making.* Journal of Neuroscience. 2015;35:2476–2484. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2410-14.2015. PMID: 25673842. Access: PubMed abstract; PMC full text available.

**Question / preparation / task / method / regions:** Human and nonhuman-primate datasets from three paradigms were compared with fixed and collapsing decision-bound models using large-scale model selection.

**Key findings:** Fixed-bound models received overall support, and the authors found that apparent support for dynamic bounds depended on paradigm or procedure; they emphasized difficulty distinguishing the models (PubMed abstract).

**Relevance:** Strong evidence against treating urgency or collapsing bounds as a default rescue of any DDM mismatch.

**Limitations stated/visible:** Comparison concerns bounds/urgency across particular datasets, not the present pulse-input reconstruction.

**Claim-ready paraphrase:** Large-scale comparisons often favored fixed bounds and showed that conclusions about urgency can depend on task and procedure (abstract).
