# ditterich2006evidence

**Citation:** Ditterich J. *Evidence for time-variant decision making.* European Journal of Neuroscience. 2006;24:3628–3641. DOI: 10.1111/j.1460-9568.2006.05221.x. PMID: 17229111. Access: PubMed abstract.

**Question / preparation / task / method / regions:** Human reaction-time random-dot motion behaviour was modelled and compared with previously recorded macaque LIP activity. Diffusion variants allowed time-varying decision dynamics.

**Key findings:** The study asked whether conventional diffusion accounts capture both speed/accuracy and decision-related neural activity and evaluated time-variant alternatives (abstract).

**Relevance:** Precedent for treating urgency or time-dependent decision policy as a real modelling alternative rather than assuming stationary bounds.

**Limitations stated/visible:** Abstract-level review does not establish which particular time-varying component maps onto the present late-response misfit.

**Claim-ready paraphrase:** Reaction-time motion data can motivate explicitly time-varying decision models when stationary diffusion descriptions are insufficient (abstract).
