# thura2012urgency

**Citation:** Thura D, Beauregard-Racine J, Fradet CW, Cisek P. *Decision making by urgency gating: theory and experimental support.* Journal of Neurophysiology. 2012;108:2912–2930. DOI: 10.1152/jn.01071.2011. PMID: 22993260. Access: PubMed abstract and author-hosted full article summary.

**Question / preparation / task / method / regions:** Human random-dot-motion behaviour under within-trial changes in coherence was used to test a policy that low-pass filters novel sensory information and multiplies it by motor-related urgency.

**Key findings:** The authors argue that a short sensory estimate plus growing urgency can account for behaviour and neural buildup otherwise attributed to long-lived accumulation (PubMed abstract; full-text Introduction/Discussion summary).

**Relevance:** Counter-evidence for a unique accumulation account and a concrete sensory-plus-response alternative under H3.

**Limitations stated/visible:** Theory and human behavioural support; does not validate urgency in these mice or show that urgency fixes the present pipeline.

**Claim-ready paraphrase:** A growing urgency signal applied to a short sensory estimate can mimic behavioural and neural signatures often attributed to evidence accumulation (abstract).
