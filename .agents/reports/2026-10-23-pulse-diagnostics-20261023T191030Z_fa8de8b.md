## Question
Why does the existing model, with or without the amendment 6 pulse gain, fail on pulse trials? Is the failure in choice, latency, response probability or their relationship? And what is the one bounded next step, or should modelling stop?

## Summary
- **The decomposition is exact.** The objective splits into three parts: response vs censored, choice given response, and RT bins.
- **A choice–RT tradeoff is demonstrated in the two mice with the most data and an RT term that covers almost all trials.** In PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, the gain that best fits choices (4–7) is 2–3× the joint optimum. The RT term rejects that gain, both in-sample and on held-out folds. In 6 of the other 8 mice the choice term is too flat to tell.
- **The mismatch is in the timing of the pulse's directional effect.** At every gain the model puts the directional effect early (0–1 s after onset) and then reverses part of it. The data show no early effect, accrual at 0.5–2 s and no reversal. This holds for the leak/urgency baseline too.
- **No common latency effect needs explaining.** The model at the fitted gains predicts almost none, and neither do the data (package D).
- **The with/against-coherence asymmetry from amendment 6 matches the control fit's own coherence-slope error.** It is the known control psychometric misfit, not a pulse effect.
- **Physical amplitude errors are too small.** Package B allows at most ×1.4, while the choice data need ×2–7. B's rounding shift accounts for at most about 0.3 pp.
- **Recommendation:** one bounded candidate, an added causal pulse time constant (F branch 1 form; B gives it no physical support). It has explicit falsifiers, and benchmarking on PSUIM6/PSUIM7 should come before anything wider.
- **Confidence:** moderate for the timing mismatch and for the tradeoff in PSUIM6 and PSUIM7; low elsewhere.

## E5: ranked mismatches
1. **Temporal shape of the directional pulse effect** (strongest).
   - The model produces the effect early and then reverses it; the data show it delayed and sustained.
   - This is present at all gains and in both baselines.
   - It explains the demonstrated choice–RT tradeoff: matching the final choice effect forces an early response burst that the RT term rejects.
2. **Excess pulse sensitivity beyond the physical input** (×2–7 needed against B's ≤ ×1.4).
   - Partly confounded with item 1: a delayed or longer pulse response could deliver the same choice effect at a lower peak gain.
3. **Control psychometric misfit at 18–36%.** It produces the with/against asymmetry. It is shared with control trials and is not pulse-specific; treat it as a caveat, not a target.
4. **Possible sign asymmetry in survival.** Weak and suspected.

There is no evidence for a common latency or engagement effect (branch 2).

### Recommendation: one falsifiable candidate (F branch 1 form)
- **Model.** Pass the pulse component through one additional causal exponential kernel with time constant tau_p (per mouse, or shared), together with the existing gain w:
  - input = no-pulse + w · (pulse ∗ exp kernel(tau_p), normalized to unit area);
  - control parameters stay frozen at fits_final;
  - tau_p = 0 is the current model.
- **Physical support.** B gives no physical support for a longer pulse response (the ME is causal and the replay is consistent). So this is a hypothesis about the internal pulse response, and it must not double-count the existing ME filter delay.
- **How it differs from what was already tested.** The secondary baseline's leak and urgency act on all evidence and on the bound, and they fail the same way (item 1). This candidate acts only on the pulse transient.
- **Predicted if correct:**
  - (i) the profile has a minimum at tau_p > 0;
  - (ii) in PSUIM6 and PSUIM7 the joint and choice-only gain optima converge;
  - (iii) the directional time course shifts later, with the late reversal (2–4.58 s window) near 0;
  - (iv) the early response burst at 0.6–1 s disappears.
- **Disconfirmed if** tau_p → 0, the w_choice/w_joint ratio stays at 2–3× in PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, or the late reversal persists at the best (w, tau_p).
- **Expected on controls.** Identical by construction: the pulse component is 0 on controls, and w = 0 reproduces the no-pulse input exactly. Any change in control predictions means an implementation error.
- **Smallest useful benchmark.** A 2-D in-sample grid on PSUIM6 and PSUIM7, followed by fold-held-out scoring on the stored folds:
  - grid: tau_p ∈ {0, 50, 100, 200, 400 ms} × the existing 23-gain grid, 50 reps, CRN;
  - measured cost of one gain evaluation at 50 reps (4 Numba threads, shared machine): PSUIM6 17.7 s, PSUIM7 39.7 s, plus 62–73 s precompute each;
  - total ≈ 5 × 23 × 57 s ≈ 1.8 h CPU at that load.
  - Per-mouse timings varied up to ×10 between runs because of machine load: DBM12 10.6 s per gain for 980 trials, against DBM15 0.77 s for 783.
  - Over the 1 h guideline. Not run; needs the coordinator's or user's go-ahead.
  - Cheaper first pass (about 25 min): tau_p ∈ {0, 100, 300 ms} × 8 gains around the choice-only optimum.
- **Alternative: stop modelling.** This is reasonable if the coordinator prefers it. With 10 mice, gains in 6–8 mice are not identified (flat choice term, sparse RT term). Any candidate can only be tested convincingly in the 2–4 mice with dense data.
