## Question
Do scheduled pulses shift response direction, and how does this break down into changes in P(right), P(left) and P(unresponded) by 5 s? Mice are the unit, on the contract's common-support cells. Pulse vs timing-matched control is included.

## Summary
- Positive (rightward) vs negative (leftward) scheduled pulses, primary set (|coh| ≤ 36%): **Δdirection = +5.84 pp, 95% t CI [1.80, 9.89], n = 10 mice**. Unadjusted p = 0.0097, Holm p = 0.019. Mouse bootstrap [2.60, 9.16].
- Components: ΔR +4.81 [0.47, 9.16], ΔL −6.88 [−10.97, −2.78], Δunresponded +2.07 [−0.36, 4.49]. Mostly a redirection of responses, possibly with a small increase in omissions under positive pulses (t CI includes 0).
- Positive vs control: Δdirection +4.86 [2.43, 7.29], Holm p = 0.0043. Control vs negative: +1.40 [−2.13, 4.92], Holm p = 0.39. All three Holm family members were estimable.
- The direction is consistent across leave-one-mouse-out, session halves, within-session halves and the pseudo-onset seed sensitivity. Zero coherence and matched seeds point the same way but are imprecise. 9 of 10 mice have positive Δdirection.
- Confidence: moderate for a nonzero directional effect of a few pp. Low for its size (wide CI with n = 10) and for any pulse-sign asymmetry.

Assignment was verified as randomized and unannounced (cfg assignment_verdict). So the positive-vs-negative assignment contrast can be worded causally, with that verdict's two qualifications: the task code read is the repository HEAD, and there are small outcome-dependent exclusions. Pulse vs control additionally depends on pseudo-onsets for UCLA controls, which D1 accepted.

## Caveats
- Only 10 mice. The t interval assumes approximately normal per-mouse estimates, and the bootstrap is a sensitivity check, not a guarantee. Mouse heterogeneity is large: one mouse is negative and two are near 0. Low power for anything beyond the primary contrast. Do not read the control − negative null as "leftward pulses have no effect". Its CI includes effects as large as the positive − control effect.
- The estimate is for the common-support population (cell weights min(n_pos, n_neg)), not all trials.
- Rig and cohort are confounded; rig is a covariate only, and no rig comparison was made or should be made from these tables.
- Stimulus caveat (package B): replay noise redraws at pulse offset. This does not affect the behavioural estimates themselves, which use scheduled assignment, but it limits mechanistic interpretation.
- Everything here is exploratory and separate from the closed primacy procedure. No timing or primacy test was run. No adjustment for RT or ME was made.
