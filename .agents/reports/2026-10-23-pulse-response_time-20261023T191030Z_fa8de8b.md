## Question
How do scheduled pulses change the joint distribution of right responses, left responses and no response over time since the (pseudo-)onset? The aspects examined are response direction, latency and response probability. It also checks whether the control alignment rule is adequate (D1).

## Summary
- **D1 accepted.** UW uses recorded sham onsets. UCLA uses A's fixed-seed pseudo-onsets, which are reproduced exactly from metadata and match the pulse-onset distribution. The grid is 0 to 4.58 s since onset, a common range inside every trial's follow-up.
- **Direction.** Pulses redirect responses. For pooled strata in the onset-eligible set, the directional contrast (dR - dL)/2 (positive minus negative) is +5.85 pp at u = 4.58 s, 95% t-interval [1.93, 9.77] across 10 mice. It is positive in 8 of 10 mice.
- **Timing of the directional mass.** It accumulates mainly between 0.5 and 2 s after onset, which is where most responses occur. It is about 0 before 0.5 s.
- **Latency and response rate.** I found no clear common effect of both pulse signs against control on response rate or latency (for example, -0.51 pp responded by 4.58 s, [-2.10, 1.09]). With 10 mice the study is underpowered, so this null should not be over-read.
- **Confidence.** Moderate for the direction effect. Everything else is low and descriptive: intervals are pointwise, with no multiplicity correction.

## Caveats
- There are 10 mice (6 for late). Intervals are pointwise, with no multiplicity correction and no p-values. Many quantities were inspected.
- Cumulative contrasts carry earlier effects forward. A late value is not evidence of retention, and onset eligibility does not identify a single integration window.
- "Where direction accrues" reflects when responses happen as well as when pulse evidence enters, so compare it with model predictions on the same weighting.
- Design choices (proposed to the coordinator, not contract changes):
  - (a) For the onset-eligible summary, A's weighting rule is re-applied to the eligible trials.
  - (b) For the common effect, a three-arm comparison uses min(n_pos, n_neg, n_ctrl) weights. This is the "min over compared arms" extension.
  - (c) The UCLA pseudo-onset pool uses |coh|, where D1's wording says "coherence". Accepted, with the justification in the d1 notes.
- Stratum curves pool rig schedules within a stratum name (UCLA 60/240 ms; UW 50/233/417 ms). Rig is used only for schedules, and no rig-level comparison is made.
