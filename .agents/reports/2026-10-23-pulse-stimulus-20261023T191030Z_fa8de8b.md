## Question
Does the replayed pulse stimulus, and its motion energy (ME), match the physical stimulus? Specifically:
(1) is the pre-onset stimulus identical with and without the pulse; (2) does any ME difference come before the
physical onset; (3) what pulse-aligned ME difference stays after the pulse; (4) how does the physical omission
compare with the DDM's average-component subtraction (`primacy.no_pulse_frames`); (5) what is known about UCLA freeze,
duration and display facts; (6) is a full replay needed, and what would it cost?

## Summary
- The replay is internally consistent. Before onset the rendered frames and the ME are bitwise identical with and
  without the pulse in all 532 replays. The ME is causal: it first differs one frame after the stimulus does. The
  replay reproduces the stored ME and the DDM's S+N. VERIFIED.
- Returning to baseline redraws every noise-dot direction. This is in the task code itself, not only in the replay.
  After the pulse the noise is a new, independent draw (pre/post noise correlation about 0). Its pulse-aligned mean
  is about 0, so it adds noise but no directional bias. VERIFIED.
- A small directional bias does exist, and it is deterministic. Rounding in the task's coherence-update code leaves
  one more or one fewer coherent dot after the return, always toward the pulse direction, and this lasts for the rest
  of the trial. The size is +0.198% coherence (UCLA |c| = 9 congruent, 18 and 36 incongruent) and +0.658% (UW |c| = 9
  and 36 incongruent). Averaged over all genuine pulses that is +0.074% (UCLA) and +0.161% (UW). VERIFIED in code and
  in all 13,547 pulse trials checked. Whether it matters for behaviour is a HYPOTHESIS for C/E.
- The UCLA replay's one-frame freezes cut its early pulse energy. The replay gives 72–78% of the 0–333 ms pulse
  energy of the no-freeze and display-loop alternatives (ratios 1.28 to 1.40). Which alternative matches acquisition
  is UNKNOWN. For UW the task frame-counter convention and the replay agree within 1%.
- Recommendation: a full physical-omission replay is not needed to interpret the existing behaviour. If the pulse gain
  is reported quantitatively, recompute the UCLA pulse with labelled alternatives. Cost is about 46 core-hours per
  convention (≈ 5.7 h on 8 cores).

## Findings

### B1. Code conventions (VERIFIED by reading code; task sources NeuRPi fcfb603, RDK-Task 6fb3878, HEAD may postdate collection)
- Frame conversion (`compute_motion_energy_full.convert_to_frame_units`):
  - onset_frame = round(onset_s·1000/16.67) + 1. This is a 1-based number; 1000/16.67 = 59.988, not 60.
  - offset = onset + round(duration·59.988).
  - `render_stimulus_deg` applies the change at 0-based index i = onset_frame − 1. Index 0 is `new_stimulus` with no
    move, so a pulse with onset_frame = 1 would never be applied (no trial has one).
  - The DDM's `to_ms` maps ms → frame int(t·60/1000). Onset index o therefore corresponds to time o/60 s, consistent
    with the recorded onset to within rounding (±8.3 ms for UCLA).
- UW off-by-one: the timing is the same, but the dot state is one move behind.
  - In NeuRPi `StimulusManager`, `initiate_stimulus` calls `new_stimulus` without drawing. Each update call moves the
    dots and then draws; the pulse is applied when `frame_counter == P`, which is the (P+1)-th move.
  - Displayed frame k therefore shows the state after k+1 moves. In the replay, index k shows the state after k moves,
    and the pulse comes at the P-th move. The replay's frame 0 was never displayed.
  - Both put the first pulse frame at time index P = 3/14/25 (50/233/417 ms).
  - Consequence: the replay's background lags one move, and the post-offset random draws shift.
    - Measured: pulse energy ratio, task convention to replay, 1.006 (0–333 ms).
    - Post-return noise correlation with the replay 0.915 (all 6,331 UW pulse trials). The residual SD of post-return
      effective coherence is 2.0%.
  - The task's dot speed is int(350/clock.get_fps()). It is 5 px/frame only if the measured fps is above 58.3; fps is
    not logged (UNKNOWN).
  - `update_coherence` and `move_dots` match NeuRPi core apart from a print statement and default arguments.
- UCLA (RDK-Task `StimDisplay.py`, `Burst_Experiment.py`, `Functions.py`, `MOCO.py`). `RandomMotion.py` differs from
  `scripts/ucla_rdk.py` only in window size, defaults and velocity scaling.
  - The 'updateBurst' loop pass changes directions without moving or drawing, and does not tick. The next pass moves
    and draws, so the rig skips no dot step. The replay instead replaces a whole 60 Hz move with the update. Every UCLA
    replay pulse is therefore "freeze, 2 moving frames, freeze": the freezes are a replay artefact.
  - Display-loop rate: StimDisplay defaults to FrameRate = 150 and changes it only on a 'frameRate' message.
    - `Functions.py` sends 'updateFrameRate', which StimDisplay does not handle.
    - `MOCO.py` sets stimFPS from Settings.csv (190) before it connects the valueChanged signal.
    - So at HEAD the loop runs at 150 passes/s unless the experimenter touched the spin box. The replay assumes 190.
      Acquisition is UNKNOWN; the 2026-10-20 author confirmation of "UCLA display settings" did not address this path.
  - Dot lifetime: at session start the task sends updateLifetime = 0.5·stimFPS = 95 passes ("Short Lifetime" is the
    default) or 38,000. The replay uses 60 frames of 60 Hz.
    - `randint(95)` consumes the RNG differently from `randint(60)`. With lifetime 95, only 12.2% of UCLA seeds keep
      the replay's initial directions.
    - Initial noise correlation with the replay is 0.769 (7,216 UCLA trials). If the rig used lifetime 95, the
      replay's per-seed hidden coherence is only partly the displayed one. UNKNOWN acquisition fact; it affects the
      noise channel, not the pulse sign.
  - Monitor refresh and vsync: vsync was off (code comment of March 2021); the refresh rate is not in the repository
    (UNKNOWN).
  - UCLA 0%: the task stimulus is ±0.1% (random sign). That is 1 coherent dot of 505, its sign given by `target`; the
    replay uses 0.
- DDM inputs (`build_ddm_inputs.py`):
  - S = c·onset_response + δ·pulse_response. The pulse response is fitted only on modal-length pulses (UCLA 3 frames,
    UW 4) and applied to all pulse lengths.
  - The 3 in-DDM UCLA pulses with 2 or 4 frames are all responses at about 0.300 s during the pulse, so their recorded
    duration is stale.
  - `primacy.no_pulse_frames` = c·onset_response + N, where N comes from the pulsed replay.

### B2/B3. Paired replays (subset of 184 trials, 532 trial × convention replays, 180 frames)
- Subset: 76 UCLA + 108 UW genuine pulses.
  - Metadata only, 3 per rig × sign × c ∈ {0, ±9, ±36, ±100} × onset stratum, seed default_rng(20261023+2).
  - Plus the 4 UCLA in-DDM trials with an atypical duration or a response during the pulse.
- Rendering identity (VERIFIED):
  - The mirror is bitwise equal to `render_stimulus_deg` in both branches, 184/184.
  - Pre-onset pixel difference is 0 and pre-onset ME difference is exactly 0, 532/532.
  - The first stimulus difference is at o (all non-pass conventions). In the pass emulations it is at o or o+1.
  - The first ME difference is always exactly 1 frame after the first stimulus difference: the temporal kernel is 0
    at lag 0. No ME difference precedes the physical onset.
  - The replayed ME matches the stored shards (max relative error 5.0e-7) and the DDM S+N (max 5.7e-5 % coherence).
- Sign-aligned ME difference (with − without pulse), equivalent % coherence, mean [95% t-CI], trials as units, replay
  convention:

  - The post-pulse difference is large per trial but not sign-aligned. VERIFIED that it is not resolvable from 0 at
    this n.
  - The minimum detectable mean is about ±1.5–2%; the exact 0.07–0.16% rounding shift is below that and is measured
    exactly in the dot-state audit instead.
  - The by-sign split shows one nominal p = 0.034 (UCLA negative pulses, late window, −1.98) among many uncorrected
    tests. Treat it as noise.
  - Every UCLA dot's position decouples after the pulse (frac_pos_diff = 1.0) because the freezes shift the move count
    by 2.
- RNG coupling (VERIFIED from code and measured):
  - The branches share the initial seed. They first consume different draws at the return-to-baseline redraw (UW and
    UCLA), and at the UCLA freeze frames, which skip lifetime draws.
  - After that, every lifetime respawn draws different positions. By the end of 3 s, positions differ for 83–100% of
    dots.
  - Equal seeds therefore do not hold future draws fixed. The "physical omission" here is one specific coupling:
    same seed and same pre-onset stream, with the stream diverging naturally after the change.
- Comparison with the DDM average-component subtraction:
  - The gap is no_pulse_frames − physical omission. Before onset it is exactly 0.
  - In the 0–333 ms window, signed gap UCLA −1.44 [−2.75, −0.12], UW −0.16 [−1.43, 1.12].
  - After 333 ms the signed gap is ≈ 0 but its per-trial SD is 8.2% (UCLA) and 6.5% (UW). This is the redraw noise,
    which the DDM puts in the "no-pulse" channel.
  - The DDM pulse component ends by 1 s (late window 0.000) and has mean 15.4 (UCLA) and 25.0 (UW) in 0–333 ms. The
    physical mean is 13.9 and 24.8.

## What B means for interpretation
- Pulse persistence: the replayed and the physical stimulus carry no sign-aligned post-pulse motion apart from the
  deterministic one-dot rounding shift (+0.07% and +0.16% averaged; at most +0.66% in specific cells). A long-lasting
  behavioural pulse effect is therefore not explained by a persisting replayed noise bias. Whether the rounding shift
  matters relative to observed effects is for C/E to test as a covariate by cell. HYPOTHESIS.
- The redraw is real on the rig, so post-pulse noise in the DDM's "without" channel is the displayed noise, as far as
  the replay matches the rig.
- Fitted pulse gain w_p:
  - The DDM component is close to the physical mean (UW 25.0 vs 24.8; UCLA 15.4 vs 13.9).
  - The UCLA pulse energy is likely understated by 22–29% relative to the freeze-free alternatives (ratios
    1.28–1.40). A UCLA w_p is inflated by roughly that factor if the rig had no freeze.
  - This changes magnitudes, not signs, and cannot by itself explain the large (~15×) excess reported earlier.
- A nonzero absolute post-pulse difference (≈ 7.8% unsigned) is redraw noise, not bias.
