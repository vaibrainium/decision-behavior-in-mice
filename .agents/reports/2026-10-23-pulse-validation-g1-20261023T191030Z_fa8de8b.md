## Question
On A's actual design, do the C and D estimators recover known effects without bias? Are their 95% intervals calibrated? What are the null rejection rate and the power of the primary test with 10 mice?

## Summary
- **C's Student-t interval across mice is adequate:** coverage is 0.93–0.975 in all six scenarios.
- **The mouse bootstrap percentile interval under-covers:** coverage is 0.85–0.925.
- **Null rejection is at or below nominal:** the primary test rejects 0.025 of the time (95% MC CI [0.008, 0.057]); the Holm family rejects 0.030 [0.011, 0.064].
- **Power of the primary test (unadjusted / Holm):**
  - δ = 3 pp: 0.29 / 0.21
  - δ = 6 pp: 0.81 / 0.64
  - δ = 10 pp: 0.995 / 0.985
- **Estimator behaviour:** C and D are unbiased to within Monte Carlo error. Probability conservation, the CIF checks, censoring and the rule that pre-onset responses are unaffected held in all 1,200 datasets. D recovers a known time-local perturbation, and C recovers a censoring-driven change in response rate.
- **Confidence:** high for the estimator and censoring logic. Moderate for interval calibration, because it depends on the assumed outcome model and normal heterogeneity across mice.

## Design (declared in the validation.py docstring before any simulation was run)
- **Trials:** the frozen trial table's primary-set trials (|coh| ≤ 36%), keeping their arms, cells, sessions, onsets, symmetric flags and the 5 s deadline. Only `response_side` and `rt_s` are replaced.
- **Base outcome model:** each mouse × signed coherence draws from its empirical control-trial multinomial (R, L, none) and its side-specific empirical control RT pools.
- **Pulse mechanism:**
  - A positive pulse flips L to R with probability q, only for responses with RT in [onset + a, onset + b]. A negative pulse mirrors this.
  - Responses before onset are therefore unaffected by construction (generator iii). The pre-onset check confirms it in every dataset.
- **Scenarios, 200 datasets each:**
  - `no_effect` (i): no pulse effect.
  - `dir3`, `dir6`, `dir10` (ii): per-mouse true Δdirection δ_m ~ N(δ, τ).
  - `censor` (iv): pulse-trial RTs after onset are delayed by +0.5 s, and those beyond 5 s become unresponded. There is no directional effect.
  - `timelocal` (v): the flip is restricted to 0.5–1.0 s after onset; δ = 6 pp at 5 s.
- **Heterogeneity rule:** τ² = SD_obs² − mean within-mouse null variance.
  - SD_obs = 5.654 pp (C's observed per-mouse SD).
  - Within-mouse null sampling variance = 15.61 pp², which gives **τ = 4.05 pp**.
- **Truth:** computed exactly from per-trial expected indicators with the estimators' own cell weights. Both estimators are linear given the design counts, so this is exact. For D's onset-eligible set it is a plug-in truth, with weights from expected eligible counts.
  - Population truth averages over the δ_m distribution.
  - Conditional truth is the mean of the current dataset's 10 mice.
- **Estimators:** called from the packages, not copied.
  - C: `behaviour.per_mouse`, `group_rows` (t interval and bootstrap with 10,000 resamples) and `holm`.
  - D: `response_time.aligned`, `estimate`, `contrast_curves` and `tci`, on a coarse grid u ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4.58} s.
- **Rates** get Clopper-Pearson 95% Monte Carlo intervals.

## Caveats
- The generators are behavioural, not mechanistic. They test estimators and intervals, not any model of pulse processing.
- Monte Carlo intervals on coverage are about ±3 percentage points at 200 datasets. The harness can rule out gross miscalibration, not small departures.
- Power figures assume the δ_m spread derived from the data (τ = 4.05). Under smaller heterogeneity, power would be higher.
- The observed 5.84 pp estimate sits near the dir6 scenario, where the Holm-adjusted primary has about 64% power. A replication at this n could therefore fail about a third of the time even if the effect is real.
